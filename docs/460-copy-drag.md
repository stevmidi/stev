# ⌥-Drag Copies Clips

**Status: design agreed 2026-11-12; built 2026-11-13 (issue #28).** In the arranger, holding ⌥ while dragging a clip by its header band, or a marquee block, drops a copy and leaves the source where it was: the Finder, Logic and Ableton gesture. It is the band move drag (`020` § Clip Band Press & Move Drag) with a different commit, and the mouse twin of `⌘/Ctrl+D` with a destination the hand picks.

## Background

The band drag already has everything a copy needs except the commit. A press on a clip's header band selects the clip and marquees its span (`ClipMoveKind::Clip`); a press inside an active marquee grabs the whole block, its ticks × its tracks (`ClipMoveKind::Marquee`). The drag is a ghost only the view knows about (`ClipMoveDrag`, `pointer_state.rs`), snapped to the arranger grid and kept on the lanes (`snapped_move_start`, `clamped_track_delta`). Release commits one undo step, `MoveClip` or `MoveRange`, and Esc cancels.

Today the only ways to copy clips are `⌘C`/`⌘V` (two keys and a cursor move) and `⌘D` (always flush after the source, on the same tracks). Putting a copy two bars later, or on another track, takes a copy, a click and a paste.

⌥ with a mouse press is free in the arranger: the only ⌥ chords there are on the arrow keys (`⌥←`/`⌥→`, the clip-edge jump). ⌥ otherwise means "coarser step" in this app (`010`); copy-on-⌥-drag is the one convention strong enough to override that, since every Mac app that drags things uses it.

## Decisions

1. **⌥ is read live and decided at release.** The drag starts as any band drag does. While it is live, ⌥ held means the release copies and ⌥ up means it moves; the user can press or release ⌥ mid-drag and the release does whatever the key says at that moment. ⌥ at press time means nothing on its own. The pointer shows the copy cursor (`egui::CursorIcon::Copy`) instead of the closed hand while a dragging, ⌥-held band drag is live, so the release's meaning is always on screen. Rejected: latching ⌥ at press time. It is simpler, but a hand that presses ⌥ a moment after the button expects a copy.
2. **Both kinds of drag copy.** A `Clip` drag copies the whole clip; a `Marquee` drag copies the block, and a clip the marquee only partly covers copies just its marqueed piece, the piece `MoveRange` would split out (region-windowed, full event list kept, as `⌘C` makes it). Muted clips copy muted. The clip being recorded stays ungrabbable, as for a move.
3. **The copy overwrites what it lands on, its own source included.** The destination is carved Ableton-style on every track the copy lands on, as `⌘D` and a paste carve. Because the source stays, a copy dropped over part of itself trims the source there: the copy wins. The copy is taken from the source before anything is carved, so it is always the whole of what was grabbed. Rejected: refusing a drop that overlaps the source.
4. **Afterwards the selection follows the copy.** The cursor goes to the copy's start, its first track is selected, the lead clip is the clip under the cursor there (the copy, by paste's cursor rule), and the marquee is the grabbed rect shifted by the drag. A second ⌥-drag inside it copies the copy, so drags chain the way `⌘D` presses do. A whole-clip copy ends exactly as a band press on the copy would leave it, the same end state a whole-clip move has. Undo puts back the selection, cursor and marquee from before the drag (`440`), and redo the one after.
5. **One edit for both kinds, built on the paste.** A copy keeps no clip id (a move does, which is why moves have two edits), so a whole-clip copy is a marquee copy over the clip's span on its one track. New `CopyRangeEdit` (`clip_edits/copy_range.rs`), a thin newtype over `PasteClipsEdit` in the shape of `DuplicateClipsEdit`: it takes the rect's slice (`Sequencer::copy_range_to_clipboard`) and lands each piece through `PasteClipsEdit::from_targets` at `start + delta_ticks` on `track + delta_tracks`. All placement, carving, freezing for redo and undo are the paste's. Its result is `EditResult::ClipsDuplicated` with the shifted rect as `new_selection`, so its workflow, its UI events and its selection already exist; build checks whether the variant wants a more general name once it has two users. *As built*: a variant of its own, `EditResult::RangeCopied` (same buckets), because `ClipsDuplicated`'s workflow leaves the cursor where it was and decision 4 moves it to the copy; `copy_range_workflow` sends the paste's shapes and then selects as a marquee move does (`select_range_workflow`). New `InputEvent::CopyRange { rect, delta_ticks, delta_tracks }` → `SequencerCommand::CopyRange`, Arranger-gated like `MoveRange`; for a `Clip` drag the view sends the clip's own span and track as the rect.
6. **Not in this design.**
   - **No keyboard twin.** `⌘←`/`⌘→` moves a marquee by keyboard; a copying `⌥⌘←`/`⌥⌘→` would collide with `⌥←`/`⌥→`, the clip-edge jump, and a keyboard-armed drag refuses ⌥ arrows today. `⌘D` is the keyboard copy.
   - **No ⌥-drag of notes in the piano roll.** The same idea in the other view (`020` § Note Move & Resize Drag), and the obvious next step; a phase of its own if wanted, designed then.
   - **A ⌥-press that never crosses the drag threshold is a plain click**: it selects the clip, or reselects the one clicked inside a marquee, as without ⌥. A drag released with no net delta copies nothing, as a move with none moves nothing (`ClipMoveDrag::has_moved`): a copy exactly on its source is pointless.

## Things to check while building

- **⌥ with a still pointer.** `MouseMoved` carries modifiers, but pressing or releasing ⌥ without moving the mouse sends no `MouseMoved`, so the drag can't take ⌥ from it alone. Read ⌥ from `ctx.input(|i| i.modifiers.alt)` each frame while a band drag is live (in `update()`, beside the pointer icon, as `InputPoller` polls ⌘ for `MoveModifierReleased`) and store it on `ClipMoveDrag`, which `finish_clip_move_drag` reads. Check that a ⌥ change repaints with the pointer still (egui should get a modifiers event from winit); otherwise the icon lags until the next motion.
- **The pointer icon order in `update()`**: the copy cursor goes where the closed hand is chosen, keyed the same way (a live, not-`via_keyboard` drag), but only once `dragging` has latched, so a ⌥-click doesn't flash it.
- **The ghost.** The source keeps drawing in place as for a move, which for a copy is also the truth after the drop; no change to `draw_clip_move_ghost` is expected. Look at it in use: if a copy and a move are too hard to tell apart in the ghost, that is a later tweak, not part of this.
- **The audio export run** (`130`): checked, nothing to do. `audio_export_command` is an allow list, so `CopyRange` is blocked during a run as `MoveRange` is, without being named.
- **The performance lane** (`450`): a band press selects the track and so leaves the lane, as for a move; nothing new expected.
- **Tests** (`copy_range.rs`, beside `duplicate_clips.rs`'s): a whole-clip copy to a later tick on the same track and to another track; a partial-marquee copy copies only the piece, with the full event list kept; a multi-track block keeps its track spacing; a copy overlapping its own source trims the source and wins; landing on another clip carves it; `None` for an empty range or one over no clip; undo puts everything back; redo lands the same ids (frozen). In the sequencer handler: the selection after a copy is the shifted rect with the copy as lead, and `⌘Z` puts back the selection from before (`440`'s test pair). In the view's pure gesture code: release with ⌥ copies, without ⌥ moves, and a ⌥ change mid-drag decides by the state at release.
- **Docs**: `020` § Clip Band Press & Move Drag (the copy commit, the ⌥ rule), `010` (the mouse bindings), `030` (the copy cursor), `050` (`CopyRangeEdit`), the Keyboard tab's hand-listed `FIXED` rows (`src/view/display/keyboard_tab.rs`: the mouse is listed there), and a `CHANGELOG.md` line.
