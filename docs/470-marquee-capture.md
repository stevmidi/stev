# The Marquee Frames a Capture

**Status: design agreed 2026-11-12; not built.** With an arranger marquee, `/` takes the capture's length (and, while running, its place) from the marquee instead of from the loop region. Over an 8-bar loop, marquee bars 3–4 and `/` commits what was played there as a 2-bar clip at bar 3. Without a marquee nothing changes: the loop frames the capture as today.

## Background

Every capture is sized by the loop today:

- **Running, new clip** (`Sequencer::build_committed_capture_clip`, `100` § What a new clip retains): the clip keeps the whole last pass and its region is the loop's remainder from the cursor, `[phase, phase + region_length)`, placed at the cursor. A linear take's region runs from the cursor through the content played.
- **Stopped, new clip** (`build_detected_phrase_clip`, `040` § Windows): the raw window is `loop_reference_length()` back from the last note-on, its start snapped by phrase detection, its end the last note-off + 2 beats; later clips round to whole bars.
- **Stopped with a lead clip**: the take session (`archive/290`), framed by the same detected start.
- **Running into a lead clip**: the overdub (`InsertCaptureEdit::from_running_capture`), the whole last pass landing where it was heard.

So a short capture inside a long loop means trimming afterwards, or `⌘L` to make the marquee the loop, which also cuts down what is heard while playing. The point of a long loop is to play two bars against all eight.

## Decisions

1. **The marquee is the capture window; the loop is the fallback.** A marquee with a tick range (`TimeSelectionRect::has_tick_range`) frames the capture; none, a zero-width one or a track-only one leaves today's behaviour exactly. Only its ticks count: the clip goes on the selected track, as now, whatever tracks the marquee spans.
2. **The command carries the marquee.** The sequencer's mirror (`Sequencer::marquee`) is for the agent bridge and is "never acted on by anything else"; every edit takes its range from its command. So `SequencerCommand::Commit` gains the view's marquee, sent from `Display`, as the other marquee commands are. The clip view has no marquee (focusing it drops the arranger's), so a `/` there sends none.
3. **Running, new clip: the marquee is a span, not just a length.** The take is kept as today (the whole last pass when looping, the whole take when linear); the marquee only sets what plays. Looping: the region is the marquee's phases, `[phase(m.start), phase(m.start) + m.len)` with `phase = (t − region_start) mod L`, and `start_tick = m.start`, so every note keeps the phase it was played at. Linear: the region is the marquee's span in arrangement time, placed at `m.start`. A length alone would leave the choice of which two bars to the cursor. While looping, a marquee not inside the loop region can't line up with a pass, so `/` refuses ("The marquee is outside the loop") instead of falling back.
4. **Stopped, new clip: the marquee is a length.** A stopped take has no place in the arrangement, so the marquee gives only how long the clip is and where it goes. The raw window is the marquee's length back from the last note-on instead of the loop's, and detection still snaps the start as today (`040`); the end is then exactly `start + m.len`: no two-beat tail, no one-bar floor, no whole-bar rounding, since the user chose the length (`220` principle 4: an edge the user placed exactly). The clip lands at `m.start`, the 8 bars of source kept outside its window as now. Enter's tempo fit on a project's only clip is unchanged.
5. **Nothing is cropped, so a forgotten marquee loses nothing.** Decisions 3 and 4 change only a new clip's region and start; its event list is what it would be without the marquee. A capture framed by a marquee the user forgot about has the whole take there, and `[`/`]` or an edge drag widens it back. This matters because undoing a commit doesn't bring the capture buffer back (`CommitClipEdit`, by decision).
6. **The marquee stays after `/`.** Clearing it wouldn't help with a forgotten one, which does its harm on the first `/`, before any clearing. After a running commit it covers the new clip exactly, so `⌘D`, `M`, `Delete`, `⌘L` and `Z` act on what was just made; and a repeated take (marquee once, then `/`, `/`, `/`) keeps its frame. It also keeps the rule that a commit never changes the selection. The undo step's before and after selections (`440`) are therefore the same marquee.
7. **The footer names the span.** Every marquee-framed capture says so in the footer, in the usual fg/fg_dim (no alarm colour): "Captured bars 3–4 (marquee)", "Inserted bars 3–4 (marquee)" for decision 9. Bars and beats are in the project's meter, as the timeline shows them. That line is the defence against a forgotten marquee. A hint *before* the press (the loop band showing the marquee's span while running, say) is held until a forgotten marquee is seen to bite in use.
8. **A new clip that doesn't fit is refused, not shortened.** Today a new clip is clamped to the room before the next clip on the track. A marquee length is deliberate, so a marquee clip that would overlap a clip on the selected track refuses ("The marquee overlaps a clip") instead of quietly coming out shorter. Whether `/` makes a new clip or inserts into the lead clip is decided as today (the lead clip under the cursor, `220` § `/` commits); the marquee doesn't change that choice.
9. **Running into a lead clip: the marquee is a punch-in (phase 2).** The overdub lands only what was played inside the marquee, still where it was heard: a note that starts inside it lands, closed at the marquee's end if it runs past; a note that starts outside doesn't. Wheel moves (`370`) land by the latch rule from their first move inside the marquee to its end, the clip's old value written back after. The marquee is intersected with the lead clip's span; a marquee that misses the clip refuses ("The marquee is outside the clip"). Unlike decisions 3–4 this does drop material, the part of the take outside the marquee, which is what a punch-in is for; the footer line (decision 7) says what landed. Logic's marquee punch-in is the precedent.
10. **Unchanged.**
    - **The stopped take session** (a stopped `/` with a lead clip): it has the clip view's window band for framing and ignores the marquee.
    - **Wheel capture's framing** (`370`): its "the notes' window" is now the marquee's window when there is one; no rule of its own is added. A take with wheel moves but no notes inside the marquee commits as `370` already says for a wheel-only take, over the marquee's span.
    - **The performance lane** (`450`): selecting it drops the marquee, so nothing changes there.
    - **The loop region, the loop flag and the playhead**: never touched, as for every capture (`220` principle 2).

## Things to check while building

- **Where `/` is matched.** It is `SequencerCommand::Commit` from `input_handler.rs`, which has no view state. Either `Display::forward_input_event` stamps the marquee onto it (as it binds the marquee commands) or the input event carries it; pick whichever keeps `/` in the keymap's `ACTIONS` unchanged. The audio-export allow list (`130`) already blocks `Commit` during a run.
- **The running region maths.** Decision 3 is `build_committed_capture_clip`'s region with `m.start` in place of the cursor and `m.len` in place of the loop remainder; the pass selection (`calculate_running_region` on the region grid, late-note relocation) must not change. Check a marquee over the loop's last bar with a note anticipating the wrap: it belongs to the next pass's downbeat, outside the marquee, as today.
- **The stopped window.** `detected_phrase_window` takes the length as a parameter already, so the marquee length replaces `loop_reference_length()` at the call; the tail, the floor and the rounding are skipped for a marquee clip. Check a marquee longer than the take (the window starts at the buffer's first note, as a long loop does now) and one shorter than the detected phrase (the end cuts into it; the rest stays as material past the window).
- **Bar labels** in the footer for a marquee that doesn't sit on bar lines (zoomed in, the grid is beats or 16ths): use the timeline's position format, not a rounded bar count.
- **Tests** (`capture.rs`, `stopped_capture.rs`): running looping, a marquee mid-loop gives that region at `m.start` with the whole pass kept; a marquee outside the loop refuses; running linear, the region is the marquee's span; stopped, the clip is exactly `m.len` at `m.start` with the source kept, detection's start unchanged, no rounding with a second clip; no marquee or a track-only one gives today's results (the existing pinned tests unchanged: `stopped_commit_window_is_the_retired_confirms` and the others in `220` § Pinned by); a marquee overlapping a clip refuses; undo removes the clip and leaves the marquee (`440`'s test pair, selection unchanged). Phase 2: notes inside land, notes outside don't, a note crossing the end is closed there, wheel moves latch to the marquee end.
- **Docs**: `100` (the marquee region), `040` § Windows (the marquee length), `220` § `/` commits (the table's marquee column), `370` (the window), `010` (`/` and the marquee), `020` § Arranger Time Selection (`/` reads it), and a `CHANGELOG.md` line.
