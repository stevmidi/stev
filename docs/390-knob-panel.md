# Knob Panel

**Status: design agreed 2026-10-31; phase 1 (the knobs) built 2026-10-31 (`feature/knob-panel`). Issue: #15.** Eight virtual knobs in a panel of their own under the track-header column, beside the docked clip view, each sending a MIDI CC the selected track chooses, so a plugin's macro controls (Spire's four macros listen on CC 14–17) can be turned with the mouse at any time, captured with `/` like the mod wheel, and seen in the clip's lower lane beside velocity, pitch bend and the mod wheel. The follow-up `370` § Out of scope names. Read `370` (wheel capture: what lands in a clip and how), `380` (the wheel lanes), `090` (what the capture buffer records) and `130` (how MIDI reaches a plugin) first.

## Background

From a discussion on 2026-10-31. `370` and `380` gave the two wheels a capture rule and a lane, and both left other CCs to "the CC-mapping follow-up". The first idea was CC lanes fed by a hardware controller (a Midi Fighter Twister) with a mapping from each CC to a plugin parameter. Two things changed it:

- **The mapping already exists, in the plugin.** A plugin either listens on fixed CCs (Spire's macros on 14–17) or has a MIDI learn of its own, and keeps the result in its own state. Stev only needs to send, record and play back the right CC number. A host-side map from a CC to a parameter ID is not needed for the plugins in use (§ Out of scope).
- **Hardware comes later, through Stev's knobs.** A controller sends from a fixed template, and Stev rewriting the controller's mapping is not the answer. The right shape is a converter: a hardware encoder drives knob *n* of the selected track, so one fixed template plays whichever track is up. That only needs Stev's own knobs to exist first, so this design builds them and leaves hardware out (§ Out of scope).

What the code does today: CCs other than CC1 arriving on the input are played live to the selected track's plugin (and MIDI Out thru) and never recorded (`090`). The clip model stores events as raw MIDI, so CC moves need no new storage. `Wheel` (`core/midi/message.rs`) is a two-variant enum, pitch bend and CC1, used by the capture buffer's filter, `Track`'s `WheelTracker` chase, `370`'s landing rule and `380`'s lanes.

## Decisions (2026-10-31)

1. **Eight knobs, mouse only.** A knob is a virtual controller: dragging it sends its CC live and records the move, as turning a hardware knob would. No keyboard control, no focus, no command in `ACTIONS`: a press in the knob panel never takes the keyboard. Eight, in two columns of four: the common size of a macro section (Serum 2 has eight macros, and eight macro knobs are usual in DAWs), with room for Spire's four and four more, and it fills the panel's width under the track headers.
2. **They act on the selected track.** The track the keyboard plays (`130`, the live-MIDI target): its CC numbers, its live output, its knob positions. With a clip open that is the clip view's track too, since the lead clip is the clip under the cursor on the selected track (`020`), so the knobs, the clip view and `/` always act on one track. No clip needs to be open: the knobs are for noodling at any time. Capture still only goes into a clip (decision 9): Stev has clip lanes and no arranger lanes (`380` decision 2).
3. **A panel of their own, beside the clip view, with the dock.** The knob panel sits under the track-header column, at its width, so the two line up; the clip view moves right by that width to make room. It goes with the clip panel (`030` § Panes): docked, it fills the space under the track headers beside the docked clip pane, whether or not a clip is open ("No clip at the cursor" moves right with the pane); maximized, it keeps the same left column beside the clip view; hidden (`⌘⌥E`, the arranger alone), it is hidden too: hiding the dock is a deliberate "the whole arranger". The app starts docked, so the knobs show by default. Two columns of four, each knob with its CC number as a label under it (`CC14`). The knobs are fitted to the panel's height (the docked pane is 40% of the lane area): they shrink to fit rather than clip, and the exact sizes are settled by hand-testing in the real panel.
4. **Each track owns eight CC numbers.** Saved with the track in the `.stev` (a new `TrackData` field, defaulting when absent). A new track, and a track from a project saved before this, starts on **CC 14–21**, the first column 14–17 and the second 18–21: undefined or general-purpose in the MIDI spec, so harmless on plugins that don't use them, and right for Spire's macros with no setup. Changing a number is undoable, like a track's output.
5. **The number is set on its label.** Double-click the `CC14` label to change the number (scrolling it was dropped in phase 1: a scroll turns a dial instead, and a scroll a few pixels off a dial would renumber the knob and rewrite its data). Valid range 0–127 except CC1, the mod wheel (it already has its lane) and the channel mode messages 120–127. No two knobs on a track share a CC: setting one to a number another knob uses is refused with a hint in the footer (`fg_dim`, no alarm colour), since both knobs would otherwise claim the same data.
6. **The knob owns its data.** Changing a knob's CC rewrites the old CC number to the new one on every move of it in all of the track's clips, as part of the same undoable edit. So fixing a wrong number after recording fixes playback, and nothing is left playing out of sight. A move of a CC that matches no knob (pasted in from another track, imported) is left alone: it plays, it has no lane (§ Out of scope).
7. **The knob shows the value in force.** While the transport plays, a knob follows its CC's value from the clip data, as playback chases it. While a knob is dragged, the hand wins: the drag's values are sent and shown, and the clip's moves for that CC are not sent to the output until release. After release, playback takes over at the next move. Values cross to the UI thread as atomics in `SharedAtomics` (no locks on the render path).
8. **A CC never resets.** Unlike the wheels, a CC has no rest: a filter cutoff "at rest" at 0 would be wrong. A stop, a seek or a loop wrap chases the value in force where the clip has data, and otherwise leaves the CC where it was, as a hardware knob would. The track saves its eight knob positions in the `.stev` and sends them to its output when the project loads, so a plugin starts where it was left. Moving a knob by hand while stopped is not undoable (like volume and pan) but does count as a change for the unsaved prompt (`060`), since it is saved.
9. **Captured like a wheel.** A knob drag goes into the capture buffer, stamped like a mod-wheel move (`090`, `150`), and `370`'s rules apply unchanged per controller: the take's window is its notes' window when it has notes, else the running pass holding its last move; each controller that moved lands from its first move to the window's end, latched, with the old value written back there; a controller that didn't move is untouched. A knob-only running take lands in the lead clip like a wheel-only one; a knob-only stopped take commits nothing.
10. **One controller type.** `Wheel` grows into a controller: pitch bend, or a CC by number (CC1 among them). The capture buffer's filter, the chase, the landing rule, the lane and the MCP reads all key on it. Pitch bend and CC1 keep their rest and reset rules; any other CC follows decision 8.
11. **The knob labels switch the lane.** `380`'s lower lane gains eight more lanes, one per knob. With a clip open, a click on a knob's `CC14` label shows that CC's lane, the label in `accent` while it is up, with the gutter's toggle colours otherwise (`380` decision 4); with no clip open there is no lane, and a single click on a label does nothing. `CycleClipLane` (`W`) cycles VEL → PB → MW → the eight knobs in reading order → VEL. The lane choice is view state, as in `380` decision 7: a knob lane stays up across lead-clip changes, and shows the new clip's track's CC in that position. The data marker of `380` decision 6 goes beside a knob's label when the lead clip has moves of its CC.
12. **A CC lane draws like the mod wheel.** 0 at the bottom, 127 at the top, rides smooth and holds stepped, folded per pixel column (`380` decisions 9–10).
13. **Deleting a span holds the value before it.** `380`'s span and `Delete` work in a CC lane, but with no rest to return to: the moves inside the span go, and the value in force just before the span holds across it; the value in force at the span's end is written back there, so the curve after it is unchanged. `⌘A` then `Delete` clears the CC from the clip, and the CC then stays where it was when playback reaches it (decision 8).

## Build (phases)

1. **The knobs.** The track's eight CC numbers and knob positions in the model and `TrackData`, undoable reassignment (the data rewrite of decision 6 included), the no-duplicates rule, the knob panel with its labels and sizing in all three clip-panel layouts (the clip pane moved right by the header width), a drag sending its CC live to the selected track's output, the positions sent on load. Useful on its own: eight always-visible knobs on a plugin's macros. Tests: the defaults and the round-trip of an old project, a reassignment rewrites the track's moves of the old CC and leaves other CCs alone, undo/redo restores both the number and the data, a duplicate is refused, the knob's value mapping (pure geometry). Docs: `030` (the knob panel, § Panes), `020` (where the knobs show, the track they act on), `060` (the new track fields), `130` (the knobs' live route), `050` (the edit). Changelog: eight knobs beside the clip view send a MIDI CC each, chosen per track.
2. **Capture and playback.** `Wheel` → the controller type (decision 10), the buffer records the knobs' moves, the chase per decision 8, the knob following playback and the hand winning a drag (decision 7), `370`'s landing rule per controller. Tests: a knob take lands per `370` (with notes, knob-only running, knob-only stopped commits nothing), a knob take never touches the other knobs or the wheels, a stop leaves a CC where it is while the wheels reset, the chase on a seek. Docs: `090`, `370` (more controllers), `150`, `050`. Changelog: turning a knob is captured with `/` like the mod wheel.
3. **The lanes.** The knob labels as lane switches, `W` cycling eleven lanes, the data markers, the drawing, deleting a span per decision 13. Tests: the cycle order, the span delete holding the value before it, `⌘A` + `Delete` clearing one CC and nothing else. Docs: `380` (four more lanes), `030`, `010` (`W`'s cycle), `280` § Not planned (drawing still not planned). Changelog: each knob's CC has a lane in the piano roll. The commit that finishes this phase closes the issue.

### Phase 1 as built (2026-10-31)

Details the decisions left open, as built: a knob's position is `None` until it is first turned, saved only once turned, and a load sends nothing for an unturned knob, so a plugin opened from an older project keeps its own macro state rather than getting CC 0. A dial starts its drag from 0, and a ⌘-click turns it back to 0 (the track header bars' reset gesture; decision 8's "a CC never resets" is about stops and seeks, not the user's hand). A scroll on a dial turns it: a scroll that started on that dial, its momentum included, never one coasting onto it from elsewhere (`030` § Knob Panel). The track owns its knobs (`Track::knobs`, `models/knobs.rs`); the sequencer mirrors the positions into `TrackMixAtomics::knob_value` for the panel. As-built detail is in `030` § Knob Panel, `020` § Views, `050` § Knob CC, `060` and `130`.

## Out of scope

- **Hardware controllers.** A converter from a hardware encoder to knob *n* of the selected track, so a fixed controller template (a Midi Fighter Twister) drives whichever track is up. The natural next step once the knobs exist. Until then a hardware CC still plays live and is never recorded, unless it is CC1.
- **A host-side map from a CC to a plugin parameter**, for a plugin with neither fixed CCs nor a MIDI learn. Decide only if such a plugin turns up in use.
- **VST3 MIDI-learn reassignments mid-session.** Stev asks a VST3 plugin's `IMidiMapping` once at load and `restartComponent` does nothing (`180`), so a plugin that changes its CC map after a MIDI learn and expects the host to ask again is only heard after a reload. Fix if a plugin in use needs it.
- **Moves of a CC no knob holds** (pasted, imported): they play and have no lane (decision 6).
- **More than eight knobs, keyboard control, fine-grained (14-bit) CCs.**
- **Drawing CC data** (`280` § Not planned), and arranger or track automation lanes (`380` decision 2).
- **MCP writes of CC rows** (`360`): the reads list them once the controller type exists; the writes stay notes-only.
