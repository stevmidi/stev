# Wheel Lane

**Status: design agreed 2026-10-30; phase 1 (the switch and the wheel lanes) built 2026-10-30 (`feature/wheel-lane`); phase 2 (the velocity readout) built 2026-10-30 (`feature/wheel-lane-velocity-readout`); phase 3 (deleting and clearing, agreed 2026-10-30) built 2026-10-30 (`feature/wheel-lane-delete`). Issue: #14.** The piano roll's velocity lane becomes one of three: velocity (VEL), pitch bend (PB) or the mod wheel (MW), picked in the gutter to its left. The two wheel lanes show the lead clip's recorded wheel data as a line, and take the keyboard to delete a span of it back to rest or clear it all; no drawing. Separately, a ⌘-drag on a note shows the velocity it is setting in the footer, whichever lane is up. Read `370` (wheel capture: what lands in a clip and how), `090` (what the capture buffer records) and `030` § the piano roll and velocity lane first.

## Background

Before wheel capture, recorded bends and mod moves only rode in with notes, so leaving them invisible was a fair trade (`090`, decided 2026-10-14: "if wheel data is ever shown, it is in an automation lane"). `370` changed that. A take now *replaces* a wheel's data from its first move to the window's end and writes the old value back there, and a mod wheel parked off its rest overwrites the clip's mod curve on every take into it. Those rules are deliberate, but with the data invisible the only checks are the ear and ⌘Z, so a take's effect can't be predicted or confirmed. This lane is the one view the 10-14 decision left room for.

## Decisions (2026-10-30)

1. **Pitch bend and the mod wheel only.** The two wheels the buffer records (`Wheel` in `core/midi/message.rs`). Other CCs wait for the CC-mapping follow-up `370` names, and would join as more lanes in the same switch.
2. **In the clip view, not the arranger.** The wheel data belongs to the clip, so the lane belongs to the piano roll, beside the notes it plays with. This is a clip's controller lane (Ableton's clip envelopes), not track automation: no arranger lanes, no data that outlives its clip.
3. **The velocity lane's area, one lane at a time.** The three share one height (`LOWER_LANE_H`, the old `VELOCITY_PANEL_H`) and the piano roll's time grid. No stacked lanes: the docked clip panel is already tight, and one lane at a time is enough to look at. No overlaying a curve on the velocity bars: two scales in one box read as neither. A fixed height, shared by all three lanes, so switching never moves the note grid: 80 px gives pitch bend about 40 px each side of centre, enough to read a curve's shape, which is the lane's job: it never draws values, only deletes them (decision 11).
4. **The switch is in the gutter.** The two piano-roll columns left of the lane (`2 * PIANO_MARGIN_W`) hold three stacked labels, `VEL`, `PB`, `MW`: the shown one in `accent`, another under the pointer `fg` with the pointing hand, the rest `fg_dim`: the header's toggle colours (`toggle_icon_color`), so a hover never reads as the shown lane (first built with the shown one in `fg`, the same as the hover; changed after hand-testing, 2026-10-30). A click picks one. No highlight box. The lane's right-aligned `VEL` label goes, since the gutter says which lane is up.
5. **A key cycles it.** One command, `CycleClipLane` (VEL → PB → MW → VEL), in `ACTIONS` with its row in `010`. Proposed default `W` (for wheel; free outside the computer keyboard, where it is a black key like every letter). Acts on the clip pane, docked or full.
6. **A marker for data.** `PB` or `MW` gets a small dot beside it when the lead clip has events for that wheel, so you know before switching whether there is anything to see. Not shown for `VEL`.
7. **The choice is view state.** A `Display` field, not saved in the project or the settings: every launch starts on `VEL`. It stays when the lead clip changes, so stepping through clips keeps showing the same lane. Never stored per clip.
8. **It never switches on its own.** A wheel take, an undo or a velocity drag leaves the lane as it is (the clip view never changes what it shows on its own, `220`).
9. **A hold stepped, a ride smooth.** A wheel has no ramps in playback: each move holds until the next, and Stev never sends a value between two moves. So a held value draws as a horizontal line, and the move that ends it as a vertical step. But moves closer than a 32nd note (`RIDE_GAP_TICKS`, measured between the moves, so zoom doesn't change it) are a ride, a wheel in motion sending every few milliseconds, and are joined by diagonals: the steps there are noise. (First built all stepped; switched after hand-testing, 2026-10-30. Drawing only, never playback.) The line enters the clip window with the value in force there (`Clip::wheels_at`, which folds in moves before the window, as playback's chase does). PB is bipolar: a faint centre line at rest (`0x2000`), bends above and below it. MW runs from 0 at the bottom to 127 at the top. Drawn in the lead clip's track colour, outside the clip's window dimmed like notes outside it (`SILENT_NOTE_SCALE`). No area fill in phase 1; try one only if the line alone reads too thin.
10. **Dense data is folded per pixel column.** Pitch bend can send hundreds of moves a bar. The renderer folds each pixel column to its min/max and draws one vertical span, so the cost follows the lane's width, not the event count (`030` § Rendering performance).
11. **Delete and clear only, no drawing.** Phase 1 shipped the lanes read-only (a press in them did nothing); after using it, deleting a span and clearing the whole wheel are the only edits wanted (2026-10-30, decisions 14–19). A curve's shape is still made by riding it (`370`'s latch rule, framed by the loop or `[`/`]`), never drawn.
12. **⌘-drag velocity works in every lane.** It starts on a note in the note grid (`020` § Velocity Drag), not in the lane, so the lane shown has no bearing on it.
13. **The footer shows the velocity during a ⌘-drag.** While the drag is held, the footer reads `Velocity 96` in `fg_dim` (the footer is where hints go). For a group, the range: `Velocity 64–110`. Shown in every lane, `VEL` included: the bars show the shape, the footer the number. Gone on release.

### Deleting and clearing (agreed 2026-10-30)

14. **The wheel lane takes the keyboard: a focus of its own.** `KeyFocus::WheelLane`, beside the pane, the track headers and the browser (`pane.rs`), so `Delete` means one thing per focus rather than one thing per selection: the same model as the track-header column, where `Delete` removes the track while the lanes carve clips (Ableton's). Only while PB or MW is shown: velocity has nothing to delete. Shown as the track headers show theirs: the docked panes' focus edge (`focus_edge_stroke`) along the lane's top, in place of its divider.
15. **In and out by the pointer and the lane.** A press in the PB/MW lane gives it the keyboard; a press-drag there also marks a span (decision 16). A press in the note grid, the key column or the timeline strip gives the keyboard back to the notes, and so does switching to VEL. Switching between PB and MW keeps the focus but drops the span, which belonged to the other wheel's curve. `Esc` drops the span first, then the focus. Leaving the clip view drops both.
16. **A span is a drag in the lane.** Its edges snap like the cursor (`snapped_tick_at`), in the clip's event ticks; tinted across the lane's height as the note marquee is (`accent` at low alpha), above the grid and under the line. View state, like the time selection: never saved, gone with the focus, on a lead-clip change, and after the delete that uses it. Separate from the note selection, which nothing in the lane touches (a commit never changes the selection either).
17. **`⌘A` marks the whole curve.** With the lane focused, the span becomes the clip's whole extent, material outside the window included (from event tick 0 to past its last event). `⌘A` with the notes focused still selects every note: the same chord, one meaning per focus.
18. **`Delete` / `⌫` returns the span to rest.** Every move of the shown wheel inside the span goes, muted ones too; the wheel is set to rest at the span's start (unless it is already at rest there), and the value that was in force at the span's end is written back there (unless it is rest), so the curve after the span is unchanged. The same result as riding the wheel at rest across the span under `370`'s landing rule, and built on it. Clearing the whole wheel is `⌘A` then `Delete`: no command of its own. No span, nothing happens. One undo step, restoring the clip's event list like the wheel takes (`050`); the lane stays as it is (decision 8).
19. **Other keys keep their meaning.** The transport, `W`, the zoom and the rest work as with the notes focused. A command that acts on notes (`M`, `Q`, `⌘D`, the arrows' note edits, copy/paste) takes the keyboard back to the notes first and acts there, as a track command does from the header column (`Action::leaves_track_headers`): the lane focus never swallows a note key.

## Build (phases)

1. **The switch and the wheel lanes.** The lane enum and its `Display` field, the gutter labels and their hit-test, the data markers, `CycleClipLane` with its keymap row, the line drawing (rides smooth, holds stepped) with the per-column fold, the window dimming. Tests: the per-column fold (pure geometry, beside the clip view's other geometry tests: a column with many moves gives its min/max, an empty column carries the held value), a ride joined smoothly and a hold stepped, the value in force at the window's start, the PB centre mapping, the keymap stays conflict-free. Docs: `030` (the gutter switch, the lane drawing), `020` (the lane choice as view state), `010` (the command), `090` (wheel data is now shown, in this lane), `370` decision 8, `280` § Not planned. Changelog: the piano roll's lower lane can show pitch bend or the mod wheel.
2. **The velocity readout.** The footer hint during a ⌘-drag, the single value and the group range. Tests: the text for one note, for a group, at the clamps. Docs: `020` § Velocity Drag, `030` § Header & Footer. Changelog: a ⌘-drag on notes shows the velocity in the footer.
3. **Deleting and clearing** (decisions 14–19). The model first: a pure `Clip` helper that returns a wheel to rest over a span (decision 18), beside the landing rule in `models/clip/wheel_landing.rs`, and its undoable `SequencerEdit` with a `SequencerCommand`; then `KeyFocus::WheelLane`, the span drag and its tint, the focus edge, `⌘A`/`Delete`/`Esc` with the lane focused, and the hand-back rule for note commands. Tests: the helper (moves inside removed, muted too; rest set at the start unless already there; the old value written back at the end unless rest; nothing outside the span changes; a span over the whole clip leaves no moves of that wheel and the other wheel untouched), undo/redo restores the curve, the span's snapping and the focus transitions (pure where they can be). Docs: `020` (the new focus, the span as view state), `010` (the lane's keys, the hand-back), `030` (the span tint, the focus edge), `050` (the edit), `090`, `370` decision 8, `280` § Not planned (drawing still not planned). Changelog: in the pitch-bend and mod-wheel lanes, drag a span and press Delete to return it to rest, or `⌘A` then Delete to clear the wheel. The commit that finishes the last open phase closes #14.

## Out of scope

- Drawing wheel data in the lane: a pencil, breakpoints, dragging a curve's values, moving or copying a span. Deleting and clearing (phase 3) are the only edits (decision 11).
- Showing what a take replaced: the old curve ghosted under the new one over the landed span, after a take or an undo. It would make `370`'s landing rule visible, but needs the replaced span kept somewhere for the view; decide after using phase 1.
- A resizable lane (dragging the divider between the notes and the lane), if 80 px proves too short in use. Its height would be kept in settings, like the browser's width, and shared by all three lanes.
- Wheel data in the arranger thumbnails.
- Other CCs, aftertouch and sustain (decision 1). Other CCs are designed in `390`: one lane per knob, in the same switch.
- Arranger or track automation lanes (decision 2).
