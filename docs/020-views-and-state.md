# ViewState Transitions

There are two primary editing surfaces plus several modal states. How this doc's rules were reached (retired views, bindings and fixes) is in `archive/020-views-and-state-history.md`.

## Views

- **`Arranger`** — default view; clips are arranged on the timeline.
- **`Clip`** — entered with SHIFT+TAB from the Arranger; shows and captures events, and edits whichever notes are selected.
  - **Having notes selected is not a view of its own.** The selection lives in the model (`Clip::event_selection`, on the `"sequencer"` thread), and `EventHandlers::publish_event_selection` (`selection.rs`) mirrors whether it is non-empty into `SharedAtomics::has_event_selection`, which the UI thread's key routing reads — `input_handler.rs` splits `ViewState::Clip` into `ClipContext::Clip` / `ClipContext::ClipWithSelection` by it, and `Display::forward_input_event` reads it to leave plain `←`/`→` to the handler (note nudge) when notes are selected. It is published from exactly the places where selection membership can change: `select_clip_event_workflow` (click), `select_all_events_workflow` (`⌘/Ctrl+A`), `select_events_in_rect_workflow` (marquee drag), `clear_event_selection_workflow` (ESC, a click on empty grid, and leaving the clip), and the `EventsModified` arm of `handle_edit_result` (the only `EditResult` variant carrying a selection — delete/duplicate, the capture insert, and their undo/redo). Not a blanket call after every command.
  - **Committing a capture never changes the selection** (`InsertCaptureEdit`): whatever was selected stays selected, and nothing new is selected — not even the committed notes. `capture.rs` has the regression tests.
  - **The clip view is a view of the lead clip, not a transport context**: entering and leaving touch neither the loop region nor the cursor, and `⌥=`/`⌥-` (stretch the clip) doesn't move the loop region either — except on the project's only clip, whose loop follows every edit to it (`220`). The *lead clip* is the clip under the cursor on the selected track (`select_clip_workflow` / `clear_clip_selection_workflow`). Whenever it changes, `change_lead_clip` first drops the old lead's event selection, republishing the mirror, then sends `UiEvent::LeadClipChanged { clip_view: Option<ClipView> }`.
  - **Two cursors, mirrored one way.** The lead clip's own cursor is reset from the transport (arranger) cursor on every arranger cursor move (`EventHandlers::change_lead_clip` / `sync_clip_selection_to_cursor_workflow` / `select_clip_span_workflow` run `sync_selected_clip_cursor_with_arranger`), but clip-view cursor moves (`set_clip_cursor_workflow`) stay in the clip. `Space` plays from the arranger cursor and `⌥Space` from the clip cursor (`010`).
  - The Display keeps the transport's cursor/region/playhead atomics and, separately, the lead clip's (`Display::lead_clip_time`, taken from every `ClipView` it receives). The clip pane reads the latter through the pane-aware `cursor_tick_atomic` & co. Its playhead is the transport playhead mapped into the clip (`lead_clip_playback_tick`, pure `pane::clip_event_tick_at`), hidden while playback is outside the clip, because the clip's own playback counter stalls then.
  - A capture into the lead clip is one undoable `InsertCaptureEdit` from either view, running or stopped (`100-running-capture.md`, `050-undo-redo.md`).
  - **`Clip` vs `Arranger` is the keyboard focus; the clip panel is layout**: the clip view is a panel shown maximized (the whole lane area) or docked below the arranger (`Display.render.clip_panel`, `⌘⌥E` — see `010`, `030` § Panes). `ClipEntered` shows it — with no lead clip too, carrying `None`, so the pane shows "No clip at the cursor" rather than the keyboard moving into a hidden clip view — and `ClipExited` hides it. Docked, both panes are live. A click in the pane without the keyboard focuses it first (`focus_pane_workflow`: into the clip view with the clip cursor left alone; back to the arranger, the event selection dropped), and a click's `MouseClickedTicks` carries the pane it landed in (`pane`), so it never depends on that focus having reached the sequencer.
  - SHIFT+TAB toggles both directions from a single `input_handler.rs::handle_shift_tab` match on `ClipContext`: from `Arranger` it sends `EnterClip`, which only shows the lead clip and **never creates one** (with no clip under the cursor the pane shows "No clip at the cursor"; `⇧⌘/Ctrl+M` inserts an empty clip, `010-keybindings.md`); from `Clip` it returns to `Arranger` (`exit_clip_workflow`, which also clears any event selection so a later re-entry starts clean). `ViewState::is_clip_view()` is true for `Clip`.
  - There is no modal capture view: the stopped `/` commits directly, a new clip (`CommitClipEdit::from_stopped_capture`) or an insert into the lead clip (`InsertCaptureEdit::from_stopped_capture`), both framed by phrase detection (`040-phrase-detection.md`, `220-capture-without-pending-view.md`). `ViewState` is session state, never persisted, so its discriminants are free to be renumbered.
- **The browser side panel is not a view** (`view/display/browser.rs`, replaced the `Project` modal 2026-10-03): a toggleable, fixed-width (`BROWSER_W`) panel on the left, hidden at startup, showing a tree of two expandable categories (both start expanded). **Projects**: the folders under `paths::project_dir()` (`list_project_folders`), each expandable to its `.stev` projects newest first and its `.mid` files (`list_folder`), then the loose files at the projects root. **Plugins** (macOS only — `BrowserTree::plugins` is `None` off macOS and the category is left out): every installed instrument plugin from the merged catalog (`130`), one flat list in the catalog's order (by name, then format) with the format as a dim suffix, then an inert "Scanning…" row while the catalog scan runs; `sync_browser_plugins` re-lists it on every show and whenever the scan reports (`pump_instrument_editors`). Its state is view-local (`Display.browser: BrowserPanel` — `visible`, the `BrowserTree` model, `scroll`; its keyboard focus is `Display::key_focus == KeyFocus::Browser`, below); the sequencer never knows it exists, so `ViewState` stays `Arranger`/`Clip` underneath. `⌘⌥B` shows and focuses it, or hides it; `⌘O` shows and focuses it (`010`). **Focus**: while it has the keyboard, `handle_input_events` offers every key to `handle_browser_key` first, which takes the arrows (all of them, whatever the modifiers — the arrows never act on two panes at once), Enter, Delete/Backspace and Esc; everything else falls through to the view underneath, so Space still plays and ⌘S still saves. A press in the panel focuses it; a press anywhere on the canvas takes the keyboard back (and focuses that pane as usual); Esc cancels a pending delete, else gives the keyboard back to the view underneath. **Keys**: Up/Down move the selection (kept on screen); Right expands a folder or category, or steps into an expanded one; Left collapses an expanded folder or category, else selects the row it sits under (a file's folder, a folder's or plugin's category); Enter toggles a category, puts the selected plugin on the selected track (`Display::put_plugin_on_track`, below), opens a project (`InputEvent::ConfirmOpenProject` → `LoadProject`, the panel keeping the keyboard so the next project is one Enter away), makes a folder current (and expands it), or imports a `.mid` onto the selected track at the cursor (`060` § MIDI clip import; the keyboard stays in the panel, so Enter on the next file replaces it in place); Delete/Backspace on a project arms a delete that a second Enter carries out (deleted by `Display` directly, `project::delete_project`, with a footer message; moving the selection cancels it). Mouse: a press selects the row, a press on a folder's disclosure triangle toggles it, a double-click toggles a folder / opens a project / imports a `.mid`, the wheel scrolls the list; a `.mid` row pressed and dragged out of the panel becomes the import drag (`BrowserPanel::press` → `GestureState::midi_drag`; ghost on the arranger lane under the pointer, imported on release, Esc cancels — `060` § MIDI clip import); a plugin row dragged out of the panel becomes the plugin drag (`GestureState::plugin_drag`, `input/plugin_drag.rs`): the track under the pointer — header or lane — gets an accent wash, and the release selects that track (`InputEvent::SelectTrack`, so the cursor stays put and the live keyboard plays what was dropped) and puts the plugin on it, Esc cancels. **Putting a plugin on a track** (`put_plugin_on_track`, `instrument.rs`): the plugin is loaded (`load_track_instrument`, editor shown, `InputEvent::SetTrackOutput { track, output: Instrument(..) }`), replacing any other — **not undoable**, the footer says "Replaced X with Y on track N" — and is a no-op when the track already has it. Going back to MIDI is not in the browser: it is routing, and lives in the track header's output menu (§ Track Header Output Chip & Menu). The output commands name their track (`SequencerCommand::SetTrackOutput { track, output }`), so a drop can route a track that isn't selected. **Current folder** (`Display.project.project_current_folder`, persisted as `SettingsData.last_project_folder`, where ⌘/Ctrl+N/S put a new project): opening a project makes its folder current (the projects root, `None`, for a loose one); Enter on a folder makes it current explicitly. The current folder and the open project are drawn with the region-coloured marker. Expansion, selection and scroll survive hiding the panel (not persisted); the tree is re-read from disk on every show and after a delete (`BrowserTree::reload`, a vanished selection handing over to the row at its old index); the first show expands the current folder and selects the open project (`select_initial`). `project_current_name` is set exclusively from `UiEvent::ProjectLoaded { clips, filename }`'s `filename` — `Some(name)` after a load, `None` after a new project — never optimistically by the key that requested it, so a ⌘/Ctrl+S after ⌘/Ctrl+N auto-names rather than overwriting what was open before. Save and New are not in the panel: ⌘/Ctrl+S saves instantly (existing name, or auto-named `<folder>_ddmmyy_NN`), ⌘/Ctrl+⇧+S auto-names a fresh copy and adopts it, ⌘/Ctrl+N creates a new project — see `060-persistence.md`. The pure tree model has `mod tests` in `browser.rs`.
- **Track header focus is not a view either** (`KeyFocus::TrackHeaders`, 2026-10-05): the arranger's track-header column is a keyboard focus target of its own, beside the lanes and the docked clip pane, so `Delete` removes the track when the headers have the keyboard and carves clips when the lanes do (Ableton's model). View-local like the browser's focus; the sequencer only sees the `RemoveTrack` it sends. **In**: a click on a track header's *empty* area, the name row included (`handle_mouse_click`'s header arm — it also selects the track, `TrackHeaderClicked`); the performance lane's header doesn't focus. The S/M buttons, the output chip, the mix bars and the `+` row act without changing the focus either way. No keyboard way in. **One focus owner**: `Display::key_focus: KeyFocus` (`pane.rs`) is `Pane` (the focused pane — lanes or clip view), `TrackHeaders` or `Browser`, so focusing one takes the keyboard from the other; `handle_input_events` offers keys to the owner's keymap first. **Effective** only while the arranger is the focused pane (`track_headers_have_keyboard`). **Keys** (`handle_track_header_key` → the pure `track_header_key`, unit-tested in `input/mod.rs`): plain `Delete`/`Backspace` sends `RemoveTrack { track_idx: None }` (refusals and footer message as for every remove; the focus stays, so presses chain); `Esc` gives the keyboard back to the lanes and does nothing else (the marquee stays); `Shift+Tab` gives it back and still does its usual show/hide. Everything else — `↑`/`↓` moving the track selection, Space, every chord — falls through to the arranger as usual. **Out**: a press anywhere outside the header column (the lanes, the timeline strip, the clip pane, the browser), `Esc`, `Shift+Tab`, showing the browser, and the arranger losing the keyboard to the clip view — detected as an edge (`Display::input_was_clip_view`, last frame's view vs. this one's), not a level, because a header click from the docked clip pane runs while `ViewState` is still `Clip` until its `FocusPane` lands. So the focus never comes back by itself: returning to the arranger lands in the lanes. The settings modal, an overlay, doesn't take it: the headers still have the keyboard when it closes. Stored, not derived (`220`'s "no stored mode-like state"): it is a focus, like the pane focus (`ViewState`) and the browser's, not a mode the user can't see — it is drawn (`030` § Panes) and dropped by any click elsewhere.
- **The settings modal is an overlay, not a view** (2026-10-06): opened with `⌘/Ctrl+,` over `Arranger` or `Clip` and closed by it again, by `Esc`, by a click outside the panel, or by a project load / new project (`UiEvent::ProjectLoaded`). `Display` owns it (`settings_modal.rs`; whether it is up is `Display::overlay == Some(Overlay::Settings)` — one `Option<Overlay>` for every modal overlay, so at most one is up and every "does an overlay own the input?" check reads one field) like the project dialogs and the output menu; `ViewState` stays `Arranger`/`Clip` underneath, so every "is the clip view open?" reader — the sequencer's included — keeps a true answer, and `view_state` keeps one writer (the sequencer). Opening resets the MIDI focus to IN PORT and each port list's cursor to its connected port — no round trip: the port lists are already current, the `"midiwatcher"` pushes `UiEvent::MidiPortsRefreshed` on its first poll and on every change. Tabs are `SettingsTab { Midi, Appearance }` (`modal_focus.rs`, beside `MidiSettingsFocus`); a tab is a variant plus a draw function. The tab is session-only view state (`SettingsModal::tab`), not persisted. While it is up it takes every key and the pointer (`handle_settings_input_event`, reached through `handle_overlay_input_event`, which dispatches on `Display::overlay` in `handle_input_events`' chain): a click on a tab switches to it, on a section header focuses that row, on a port row focuses it with the cursor there (double-click = Enter), on the offset row focuses it, on a theme applies it; the wheel scrolls the theme list. Lists are windows (each `PortList`'s scroll, `SettingsModal::theme_scroll`) that move only as far as it takes to keep the cursor in view (`browser::scroll_to_show`), so a click never makes them jump.
  - **MIDI tab** (`Display.midi: MidiSettingsState` — an input and an output `PortList` indexed by `PortSide`, the focus, the offset mirror; `midi_state.rs`, unit-tested): Enter applies the port under the cursor at once (`PortList::connect` sends on the list's reconnect channel to the `"midiwatcher"`, which makes it the wanted port, and updates the list's `current`) and persists both connected ports through `InputEvent::ConfirmMidiPorts`. Only Enter — moving the cursor would otherwise send notes to random gear. The offset applies and saves on every ↑/↓ nudge (`InputEvent::SetMidiOutOffset`). Every settings write runs on the `"sequencer"` thread (`settings::update_settings`), none on the render thread. The port lists refresh live from `UiEvent::MidiPortsRefreshed` (pushed by `"midiwatcher"` when its 2 s poll sees the lists change). Active devices are indicated by a green left-bar + green text; a configured device that is not currently plugged in appears as a dimmed ghost row (`○ name (not connected)`).
  - **Appearance tab**: a list of every palette in `view::theme::palettes::THEMES`. No cursor distinct from the live theme: ↑/↓ (`Display::step_theme`, wrapping) or a click (`apply_theme`) set the active theme and, when that changes it, save it into `SettingsData.theme_index` (`InputEvent::SaveTheme`, written on the `"sequencer"` thread), so the theme on screen is always the saved one — applied at startup via `theme::set_active_theme(settings.theme_index)` in `main.rs`. No default mirror, no `DEFAULT` tag. Shapes hold no colour of their own (`ClipShape`/`EventShape::color()` read the active theme at paint time), so the next frame repaints in the new palette.
- **The help overlay is an overlay, not a view** (`help_overlay.rs`, 2026-10-07), the settings modal's twin: opened with `?` or a click on the header's `?` chip over `Arranger` or `Clip`, closed by `?`, `Esc`, any press (inside the panel too — it has nothing to click) or a project load / new project (`UiEvent::ProjectLoaded`). `Display::overlay` is `Some(Overlay::Help)` while it is up and `Display::help` holds the page's `scroll`; `ViewState` and `key_focus` underneath are untouched, so it closes back to exactly what was there. While it is up it takes every key and the pointer (`handle_help_input_event`, `input/help.rs`, reached through `handle_overlay_input_event` in `handle_input_events`' chain and in `try_consume_as_modal`); only the wheel acts, scrolling a page taller than the window. The content is the `HELP` table — columns of titled sections of `(keys, action)` rows, data not code: a new binding is documented by adding a row. The section titles of the pane with the keyboard (`HelpSection::pane`) are accented.
- **ESC is the universal "never mind" key**: the settings modal closes on ESC (as on `⌘/Ctrl+,`), leaving the view underneath as it was, in `Display::handle_settings_input_event`; the help overlay likewise (as on `?`); so does the track header's output menu (any key closes it; ESC does nothing else); the browser panel's Esc cancels a pending delete, else gives the keyboard back — the same role ESC plays clearing an event selection in `Clip`. SHIFT+ENTER is unbound application-wide; do not reintroduce it as a close/back chord. SHIFT+TAB inside the settings modal is just TAB (the MIDI tab's row cycle), since the modal consumes input before the view-level bindings.

## Mouse-Driven Cursor Placement

- **Supported in `Arranger` and `Clip`** — every view (the settings modal swallows the pointer). `Display::cursor_grid_ticks()` returns the snap resolution per view: `Arranger` → the zoom-adaptive snap, the finest *visible* grid tier (`GridTiers::snap_ticks`, `view/display/grid.rs` — a beat at the default zoom, down to a 16th zoomed in, whole bars zoomed out; see `030-ui-design.md` § Arranger Navigation), `Clip` → the same zoom-adaptive snap on the piano-roll surface (`GridSurface::PianoRoll`, down to a 256th; see `030` § Grid Hierarchy). The plain arrow keys step the cursor at this same resolution: in the arranger `forward_input_event` resolves the step view-side (the zoom is view-local) into `InputEvent::MoveCursorByGrid { step_ticks }` → `TransportCommand::MoveCursor`, and in `Clip` likewise into `MoveCursorByGrid` → `SequencerCommand::NudgeClipCursorByGrid { step_ticks }` → `Sequencer::grid_snapped_selected_clip_cursor_tick`, which walks one boundary via `core::time::step_to_grid` (with notes selected the plain arrows nudge them instead — a fixed 64th, not the grid) — either way one `cursor_grid_ticks()` boundary per press, so a key press advances exactly as far as the mouse's nearest-grid-line snap. The mouse still differs in kind, not resolution: it lands on whichever grid line is nearest the pointer (`core::time::snap_to_grid`) rather than always stepping forward/back by one.
- **The cursor line reflects the pointer and nothing else**: `draw_cursor_line` (`overlays.rs`) draws one dim full-height line — `cursor_line_color()` / `CURSOR_LINE_W`, a neutral `theme::fg()` tone (~0.45 brightness) at 0.75px — at the pointer's snapped tick, in the pane the pointer is over. It reads as "the cursor, if you click here". The committed cursor is the timeline's play triangle (and, in the arranger, the selected-track row accent); the line never falls back to it — that fallback made the line flash to the committed tick whenever the pointer crossed the gap between the panes.
  - **Mouse**: `InputEvent::MouseMoved` over a pane (its lanes or its timeline strip, `is_mouse_inside_pane`) sets `Display.gesture.hover_cursor` — the tick and the pane it was measured in (view-local, snapped via `Display::snapped_tick_at`) and never touches the committed transport/clip cursor. Anywhere else (the gap between the panes, the header, the status bar) it is `None` and no pane draws a line. There is no pointer-leave event, so it keeps its last value while the pointer is outside the window.
  - **Keyboard**: the arrows move the committed cursor, not the line; the line stays at the pointer.
  - **Pinned during a time-selection drag**: `Display::update_hover_cursor_tick` (`gestures.rs`) no-ops while `gesture.time_selection_anchor.is_some()`, mirroring the same-shaped guard on `update_clip_resize_hover`/`update_track_mix_hover`. Without it the line would slide along with the pointer for the whole drag, duplicating the selection's own moving edge; pinned at the press tick, it reads as the insert marker the drag is anchored to (see "Press keeps its existing meaning" below) while the range arrows alone show the live range.
- **Click commits the snapped position**: `InputEvent::MouseClicked` is snapped and forwarded as `InputEvent::MouseClickedTicks`. `ViewState::Arranger` sends `TransportCommand::SetCursorAndSelectClip`; `Clip` sends `SequencerCommand::CommitClipClickByTicks` (same path used by keyboard cursor placement) *and* `SequencerCommand::SelectClipEvent` with whatever event (if any) the click hit — cursor placement and event selection are independent effects of the same click.
- **Timeline click places the cursor and nothing else**: a press on a pane's timeline strip (inside the pane, above its lanes — `is_mouse_inside_pane` && !`is_mouse_inside_clip_content`; the gutter corner is ignored) is handled first in `handle_mouse_click` and sends `InputEvent::SetCursorTick { pane, tick }`, snapped like a lane click. Arranger → `TransportCommand::SetCursorAndSelectClip` without the `SelectTrackAt`, so the selected track stays, the lead clip re-syncs to the clip under the new cursor and the marquee collapses — exactly an arrow-key move (the marquee is cursor-anchored and the lead clip is by definition the clip under the cursor, so neither can survive a cursor move). Clip view → `SequencerCommand::CommitClipClickByTicks` without the `SelectClipEvent`, so the note selection is kept: reposition the clip cursor (and so `⌥Space`'s start) without losing the selection. No time-selection or event-marquee anchor, no drag: a drag on the strip does nothing (scrub / loop-region drag are the obvious later uses). The whole strip counts, region band included. During playback it moves the start position like any cursor placement, never the running playhead. Where the cursor line shows (the pointer over a pane, timeline included), a click puts the cursor on it.
- **A press acts only on the grid**: the initial press is gated on `Display::is_mouse_inside_grid` — the pane's lanes (`is_mouse_inside_clip_content`) within `content_x_on_screen`, so a click on the piano key column (or the right padding) neither moves the clip cursor, starts the event marquee nor hits a note scrolled under the keys, and the same holds in the arranger. **A plain press on an arranger track header** (left of `content_origin_x`, off its S/M buttons and mix bars, checked after those and ahead of the Shift+click arm) sends `InputEvent::TrackHeaderClicked { track_idx, performance_lane_hit }` and returns: it selects that row (`select_arranger_row`, shared with the lane click) and nothing else — no time lies under a header, so the cursor stays put and no marquee starts (a changed track still clears an existing marquee via `sync_time_selection_to_cursor`). Every clip-pane gesture — velocity drag, note drag, note hover, the marquee/cursor fall-through, the double-click insert — gates on it, so a new one shouldn't hand-roll the x check. The one clip-pane press outside the grid is the **octave-legend zoom drag**, checked just before that gate (`is_on_octave_legend`, `030` § Piano Roll).
- **No modifier-key behavior for cursor placement itself.** The two modifier-gated mouse gestures in the codebase are both distinct gestures checked *before* the cursor/selection click logic runs, not modifiers on it: the ⌘/Ctrl+drag velocity edit in `Clip` ("Velocity Drag" below), and the Arranger Shift+click marquee extension ("`SHIFT+click`" under "Arranger Time Selection" below) — which deliberately does *not* place the cursor, since the cursor is the anchor it extends from.
- `TransportCommand::SetCursorAndSelectClip` and `TransportCommand::MoveCursor` share clip re-selection logic via `EventHandlers::sync_clip_selection_to_cursor_workflow` — extend that helper rather than duplicating the clip-resync block if a third cursor-setting path is added.

## Arranger Time Selection — the Marquee

- **Arranger only, gesture starts in the clip lanes**: press-drag-release inside the clip area defines a 2D marquee — a tick range *and* a track range. The timeline strip never starts or grabs a marquee (a click there only places the cursor, see "Timeline click" below); it is where the tick range is *rendered* (as the two inward-pointing arrow glyphs), not where it is grabbed. Unlike the clip-view event marquee, no separate rectangle is drawn — the arranger already tints selected content (clip-body sub-rects + gap fills, see "Visual" below), so the marquee's track range simply narrows which tracks that tint covers.
- **View-local state, never shared or undoable**: `Display.gesture.time_selection: Option<TimeSelectionRect>` (`core::input_event::TimeSelectionRect { start, end, track_start, track_end }`) holds the normalized tick range × inclusive track range. `Display.gesture.time_selection_anchor: Option<(i32, usize)>` holds the drag anchor `(tick, track)` — `Some` only while the primary button is held, so it doubles as the drag-active flag rather than a separate `bool`. No `SharedAtomics` entry, no `SequencerEdit`: a selection is not a data mutation (see `050-undo-redo.md`).
- **Snapping reuses the cursor grid; the track anchor reuses `track_idx_at`**: the tick anchor and moving edge go through `core::time::snap_to_grid` with `Display::cursor_grid_ticks()` (the zoom-adaptive snap in `Arranger`); the track anchor and live track resolve via `Display::track_idx_at(y)`. A press landing on the performance-lane row (above the track lanes, where `track_idx_at` returns `None`) clamps to track 0 for the *anchor* — mirroring `track_idx_at`'s own bottom-edge clamp; the *live* track during a drag instead falls back to whatever the anchor already was, so briefly dragging back up over the timeline/performance-lane row mid-gesture doesn't snap the span down to track 0.
- **The collapsed state is the cursor, and is never stored**: `time_selection` is `Some` only while a real drag has moved off *both* anchors — a collapsed pair is not a representable state. `draw_time_selection` derives the collapsed right-pointing play-triangle glyph from `render_cursor_tick()` instead, so it tracks every cursor move (arrow keys, viewport paging, clicks) with no syncing at all, and stays in step with the cursor line drawn in the grid. Do not reintroduce a stored `(tick, tick)` collapsed pair: it makes ⌘L keyboard-vs-mouse behavior diverge, because only the mouse path would re-anchor it. The same collapsed triangle is drawn in the clip pane (`draw_time_selection`'s view guard admits it) — the clip pane ignores `time_selection` (it stays alive under a docked panel while the arranger has the focus, and a click that moves the focus into the clip pane clears it, as clip entry does), so it only ever hits this branch, and the cursor there is likewise the playback start position. Collapsing requires returning to **both** the tick and track anchor: a drag that changes only the track row (`tick_x == anchor_tick`, `track_idx != anchor_track`) stays `Some`, with `start == end` — a real, zero-tick-width selection rather than a collapse. This is the one way to select "these tracks, at this instant" without also carving out a time range, and it's what `⌘/Ctrl+E` (Split) uses to split several tracks at one point (see its own bullet below). The content-editing ops (`Delete`/`M`/`⌘C`/`⌘X`/`⌘D`) already guard `end <= start` at their own construction (`DeleteInRangeEdit`/`MuteInRangeEdit`/`DuplicateClipsEdit`/`copy_range_to_clipboard` `build`), so a zero-width selection harmlessly no-ops there instead of touching content — matching the fact that a point in time has no content to act on. `draw_selected_track_cursor` (`overlays.rs`) reflects a multi-track selection by spanning its accent across `track_start..=track_end` instead of just `selected_track_idx`, so the expansion is visible as you drag — but only while the marquee stays zero-tick-width; see the next bullet.
- **The selection is anchored to the cursor and the selected track; either moving collapses it**: `Display::sync_time_selection_to_cursor()` (called from `update()` beside the other sync passes) latches the cursor tick *and* `selected_track_idx`, clearing `time_selection` when either changes, skipping the check while a drag is in progress — a drag sets the cursor at its own anchor and must not wipe the range it is building; the guard also shields against `UiEvent::TrackSelected` for the drag's own click landing a frame or two late (it round-trips through the sequencer thread), since the anchor is still set when it arrives. Watch both here rather than enumerating cursor- or track-moving keys in `forward_input_event`: that covers every present and future path (arrow-key cursor nav, `⌘/Ctrl+L`, ↑/↓ track nav, a click) with one rule. Playback is unaffected, since it advances `playback_tick`, not `cursor_tick`.
- **The selected-track cursor hides once the marquee has real tick width**: `draw_selected_track_cursor` skips drawing when `time_selection.has_tick_range()` — the marquee's own tint (see "Visual" below) already marks the selected span at that point, so the accent sitting at one edge of it would just be a stray leftover. A purely vertical drag (tracks only, no tick width — "these tracks, at this instant" above) leaves it visible, since that shape has no tint of its own to carry the meaning. It reappears the moment `time_selection` drops back to `None` — Esc, a fresh click, or the cursor/track re-anchor above all do that.
- **`SHIFT+L/R` is the keyboard drag — single-track by default**: `Display::nudge_time_selection_edge()` moves the free tick edge one grid step per press, so ⇧← is a drag to the left and ⇧→ a drag to the right. It is bound in `Display::forward_input_event` (like `⌘/Ctrl+L`, because the operand is view-local) and consumed there, so no `KeyPressed` reaches `input_handler.rs`. Deliberately no new tick-anchor field: a selection is always cursor-anchored (a drag places the cursor at its anchor, and any later cursor move collapses the range), so the edge sitting on the cursor *is* the anchor and the other one is free; with no selection the cursor anchors a fresh gesture. Collapsing back onto the anchor clears the selection **only if the track span is also back at its own anchor** (`self.selected_track_idx`) — the same both-axes rule `extend_time_selection` uses for a mouse drag, so nudging the tick edge home after a `⇧↑`/`⇧↓` track-range extension leaves that track range in place instead of wiping it out. Extending the tick range via keyboard leaves the track range untouched (see `SHIFT+U/D` below for its track-range twin); starting a fresh selection defaults `track_start`/`track_end` to `self.selected_track_idx` (the currently selected track only).
- **`SHIFT+U/D` is the same keyboard drag for the track span**: `Display::nudge_time_selection_track()` mirrors `nudge_time_selection_edge` with track and tick roles swapped — it moves the free track edge one row per press, so ⇧↑ extends upward and ⇧↓ downward. Bound alongside `SHIFT+L/R` in `Display::forward_input_event` for the same reason (the operand is view-local); only the *unshifted* `↑`/`↓` still reach `input_handler.rs`'s `handle_prev_next_keys` (track cycling — see "Tracks and Regions" below). No new track-anchor field either: a track selection is anchored to `selected_track_idx`, so the row sitting on it *is* the anchor and the other is free; with no selection the selected track anchors a fresh gesture. Collapsing back onto the anchor clears the selection only if the tick span is *also* back at the cursor (same both-axes rule as `SHIFT+L/R`'s collapse, above) — an independent `⇧←`/`⇧→` tick extension survives a `⇧↑`/`⇧↓` track edge returning home. The tick range has no keyboard-driven anchor to extend here: extending an existing selection leaves the tick range untouched; starting a fresh selection defaults it to the current cursor tick (zero tick width) — a mouse drag is needed to span a real tick range from a keyboard-only gesture. The loop region has no direct-to-cursor shortcut; only `⌘/Ctrl+L` sets it.
- **`⌥←`/`⌥→` is coarse cursor navigation — jump to the next clip edge — and `⇧⌥←`/`⇧⌥→` is the same step applied to the marquee's free edge**: the text-editor word-jump / select-word pair, and the everyday keyboard way to build the band-press end state (cursor on a clip's start, `⇧⌥→` once ⇒ exactly that clip's span, so `⌘/Ctrl+L`, `⌘/Ctrl+D`, `Delete`, `M`, `⌘C`/`⌘X` all behave as after a band press). *Edges* are every clip start **and** end on the **selected track** (not all tracks — `↑`/`↓` already moves across tracks, and the marquee the shifted form builds lives on the selected track anyway), plus tick 0 so a leftward jump from before the first clip still lands; the recording clip counts, its live end being a real edge. The target is the nearest edge *strictly* beyond the starting point in that direction — strict so repeated presses walk, and "start and end both count" so pressing from a clip's end selects the gap up to the next clip's start and a second press takes that clip. Nothing that way ⇒ no-op. The landing tick is **not** snapped to the cursor grid: the edge is the point (a recorded clip's end need not sit on a beat). Pure core `next_clip_edge(edges, from, direction)` (`input/gestures.rs`, unit-tested) over the view-owned `render.clip_shapes`, which is why both are bound in `Display::forward_input_event` rather than the handler. The plain jump (`Display::jump_cursor_to_clip_edge`, gated `alt && !shift && !command`) sends `InputEvent::SetCursorTick { tick }` → `input_handler.rs` → `TransportCommand::SetCursorAndSelectClip` — the click-to-place path, minus the track select — so the clip selection re-syncs and any marquee collapses via `sync_time_selection_to_cursor` exactly as after a lane click; the cursor is never moved view-locally. The shifted form (`Display::extend_time_selection_to_clip_edge`, gated `alt && shift && !command`) is `nudge_time_selection_edge` with a different step: same cursor anchor, same free-edge rule, same both-axes collapse rule (walking the free edge back onto the cursor clears the selection), same untouched track span, and it starts a fresh selection from the cursor when there is none — the free edge, not the cursor, is what the next edge is measured from, so repeated presses walk edge to edge. Nothing is sent. ⌥ was chosen over ⌘/Ctrl for this — and the keyboard marquee *move* rebound the other way — because this is the everyday key and ⌥-arrows is the coarser-cursor-step convention; see "Clip Band Press & Move Drag" below.
- **`SHIFT+click` is the one-click drag — the classic "click the anchor, Shift+click the far corner" marquee**: `Display::extend_time_selection_to_click(x, y)` (`input/gestures.rs`) sets `time_selection` to the rectangle between the anchor and the click on *both* axes at once, exactly the rect a mouse drag from the anchor to that point would build. Bound as an early arm in `MouseClicked` (`input/mod.rs`) — Arranger only, gated on `modifiers.shift` and `is_mouse_inside_clip_content`, placed after the track-header button/mix-bar hit-tests but *before* `clip_edge_at`/`clip_band_at`, so a Shift+click on a clip extends the selection instead of arming a resize or move drag — and consumed there: it never reaches `begin_time_selection` (no drag anchor is set, so the following `MouseMoved`/`MouseReleased` do nothing) and is never forwarded as `MouseClickedTicks`. **Nothing is sent** — the cursor and `selected_track_idx` must stay exactly where they are, since `sync_time_selection_to_cursor` would otherwise collapse the fresh range a frame later. **The anchor is the current selection itself** — `time_selection`, or the cursor tick × `selected_track_idx` when there is none — and nothing else is stored, so a selection built by a band press, a mouse drag, the keyboard nudges or an earlier Shift+click all extend identically (a band press and a left-to-right drag over the same clip leave the *same* state — cursor on the clip start, clip span selected — and must behave the same afterwards). Per axis (`extended_edges`, `gestures.rs`): a click *outside* the selection extends it to the click — the hull, so a band-pressed or dragged clip stays inside when you Shift+click *left* of it (`[click, clip.end]`, not `[click, clip.start]`, which is the gap before the clip with the clip itself dropped) — the Ableton / Pro Tools "extend the selection toward the click" behaviour; a click *inside* moves the edge that is **not** sitting on the cursor (tick axis) / `selected_track_idx` (track axis) — the free edge, exactly what `⇧←`/`⇧→` move, so pulling a selection back in works the same by click as by key, and a Shift+click exactly on the pinned edge collapses it just as nudging the free edge home does — or the *nearer* edge when the cursor is on neither (a selection already extended past its anchor on both sides). Repeated Shift+clicks therefore grow outward and shrink from the free side; a plain click resets everything. Do not add a stored anchor: it gives a band press and a hand-dragged range over the same clip different anchors. The click side mirrors the plain-click split between band and body (see "Clip Band Press & Move Drag" below): a Shift+click on a clip's **header band means the whole clip** (its span is the target, so the clip ends up covered whole — from before it → up to its end, from after it → from its start, from inside it → its own span), a Shift+click in a clip **body or an empty lane means that point**, snapped to the cursor grid like every other tick here. The track edge is the clicked row either way (`track_idx_at`, falling back to the selected track on the performance-lane row like `extend_time_selection`'s wander rule). Collapsing to no width on both axes clears the selection (same both-axes rule as the drags). Note the keyboard nudges keep their own cursor-pinned model: after a Shift+click has extended a selection past the cursor on both sides, `⇧←`/`⇧→` restart from the cursor rather than picking the nearer edge — a pre-existing property of the nudges, left alone. Pure core: `shift_click_selection(selection, cursor_tick, selected_track, span, track_idx)` over `extended_edges(start, end, pinned, span)`, with unit tests (including the band-press/drag leftward regression and the inside-click free-edge cases) beside `nudged_tick_selection`'s.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+A` is Select All — the marquee that surrounds every clip**: `Display::select_all_clips()` (`input/gestures.rs`) sets `time_selection` to the hull of every clip on both axes — earliest start → latest end, lowest → highest track holding a clip (pure core `clip_hull_selection(clips)`, unit-tested) — exactly the rect a mouse drag from the first clip's top-left corner to the last clip's bottom-right corner would build, so every marquee op (`⌘/Ctrl+D`, `Delete`, `⌘/Ctrl+Delete`, `⌘C`/`⌘X`, `M`, `⌘/Ctrl+L`) then acts on the whole arrangement. Bound in `Display::forward_input_event` (Arranger only, exact chord — `command && !shift && !alt`, a stray modifier refuses) for the same reason as `SHIFT+click`: both the operand (the view-owned `render.clip_shapes`) and the result are view-local, and **nothing is sent** — the cursor and `selected_track_idx` stay exactly where they are, which is what keeps `sync_time_selection_to_cursor` from collapsing the fresh rect a frame later, and keeps the armed track unchanged. The hull hugs the clips rather than the lane edges (empty outer tracks and the gap before the first clip are excluded — a `⌘/Ctrl+D` or `⌘/Ctrl+Delete` right after acts on content, not leading silence), and its edges are the clip edges unsnapped, as for `⇧⌥←`/`⇧⌥→`; the recording clip counts, its live end being a real edge. No clips ⇒ no-op, selection kept. Like a Shift+click that reaches past the cursor on both sides, the result is generally *not* cursor-anchored — the keyboard nudges (`⇧←`/`⇧→`, `⇧↑`/`⇧↓`) restart from the cursor afterwards, the same pre-existing nudge property noted above; Esc or a plain click clears it as usual. Consumed under a keyboard-armed marquee nudge like every other `⌘/Ctrl+X` chord (first arm). The same chord in `Clip` falls through to `input_handler.rs` and selects all events instead — see "Reserved Insert Spans and Event Selection" below.
- **Press keeps its existing meaning**: the press edge still commits cursor placement and clip selection via `MouseClickedTicks`; it merely also anchors the selection. Dragging therefore moves the cursor to the selection start, matching Ableton's insert marker.
- **A drag survives vertical wander**: extension is deliberately *not* gated on `is_mouse_inside_clip_content`. Only the initial press is area-gated; once anchored, leaving the lanes vertically must not cancel the gesture (the track anchor fallback above is what keeps the *track range* well-defined through that same wander).
- **Release drops the anchor only**: `InputEvent::MouseReleased` carries no coordinates — the poller emits the `MouseMoved` for the same position earlier in the same batch, so the final boundary is already committed. `time_selection_anchor` clears.
- **Cleared on leaving the arranger**: `Display::clear_gestures()` (the hover cursor, `clear_time_selection()` and every drag) runs on `ClipEntered`, `ClipExited`, and `ProjectLoaded`. A selection is scoped to a single arranger visit.
- **Esc cancels it, mid-drag or already committed**: bound in `Display::forward_input_event` (Arranger only) and calls `clear_time_selection()` — the standard cancel-this-gesture chord (Excel/Photoshop/VS Code all treat Esc this way). No cursor/track restoration is needed: the committed cursor tick and `selected_track_idx` never leave the drag's anchor in the first place ("Press keeps its existing meaning" above), so dropping `time_selection` alone leaves `draw_selected_track_cursor` reading the anchor. Also drops the drag anchor mid-gesture, so a still-held mouse button doesn't resume extending a selection until the next press.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+L` is bound in the view, dispatched in the handler**: `Display::forward_input_event` matches the key and translates it to `InputEvent::SetRegionToTimeSelectionOrToggleLoop { time_bounds: self.gesture.time_selection.map(|r| (r.start, r.end)) }` — extracting just the tick range; the loop region has no track concept. `input_handler.rs` holds the single dispatch arm and owns the *precedence* rule: `Some` bounds go straight to `TransportCommand::SetRegionOrToggleLoop` (already resolved; moves the region and re-enables looping, or toggles looping if the region already sits there), `None` sends `TransportCommand::ToggleLoop` (a bare `loop_enabled` flip, region band untouched). The selected clip is not an operand — see `010-keybindings.md` for why. Keep this shape — do **not** re-send a raw `KeyPressed` through the channel for a second handler to re-match, and do **not** promote the time selection to `SharedAtomics` just to move the binding back: independent atomic pairs tear (hence `region_bounds_snapshot()`'s retry loop), and it buys nothing functionally.

### Which operations respect the marquee's track range, and which bypass it

Every range-scoped key below reads `self.gesture.time_selection`, but they split into two groups. **Bypass** (tick range only, always every track): `⌘/Ctrl+L`, `⌘/Ctrl+I` (Insert Silence), `⌘/Ctrl+Delete` (Delete Time), `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+C`/`Shift+⌘/Ctrl+X`/`Shift+⌘/Ctrl+D`. **Respect** (tick range × the marqueed tracks only): `Delete`/`Backspace`, `M`, `⌘/Ctrl+E` (Split), plain `⌘/Ctrl+C`/`⌘/Ctrl+X`, plain `⌘/Ctrl+D`, `⌘/Ctrl+J` (Merge Clips). The bypasses are not a style choice: `⌘I`/`⌘Delete` compose `InsertSilenceEdit`/`DeleteInRangeEdit` respectively, and the `Shift+⌘` trio bypass the marquee's *track* range specifically — both touch *every* clip at/after a tick on *every* track by the same amount so the tracks stay aligned on the shared timeline, scoping that to a track subset would slide the marqueed tracks out of sync with the rest. Note `⌘I`/`⌘Delete` don't follow the `Shift+⌘` convention the other three bypasses use — there's no marquee-scoped variant of either to need distinguishing from, so the plain chord was free; `Shift` is not actually read by either arm, so `⌘+Shift+I`/`⌘+Shift+Delete` fire the same bypass too. `D` is the one key whose plain and `Shift` chords are *different operations*, not the same operation at two scopes: plain `⌘/Ctrl+D` is "Duplicate Clips" (a respecting, overwriting paste), `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+D` is "Duplicate Time" (a bypassing, space-inserting one) — see their bullet below. There is no bare `D` (plain Duplicate) binding at all — those two cover every duplicate need between them; `D` with no modifier does nothing in the Arranger.

- **`Delete`/`Backspace` and `M` are both marquee-only**: both are bound in `Display::forward_input_event` (Arranger only; `M` is intercepted ahead of the bare `Key::M` arm in `input_handler.rs`, which handles `M` for the other views — see below), the same no-single-clip-fallback shape as plain `⌘/Ctrl+D`: the view only sends `InputEvent::RemoveClipsInSelection { rect }` → `SequencerCommand::RemoveClips { rect }` / `InputEvent::MuteClipsInSelection { rect }` → `SequencerCommand::MuteClipsInRange { rect }` when a time selection is `Some`; with no selection the press is consumed and nothing is sent. There is no selected-clip fallback (on Delete or either `⌘/Ctrl+X` form): a clip is only ever deleted through a tick range, so a stray Delete or Cut with nothing marqueed can't wipe a whole clip. Do not reintroduce it; to delete one clip, band-press it (which marquees exactly its span) or build the span with `⇧⌥→`, then Delete. Both keys no-op when there is nothing to act on. The region is not involved at all.
  - **`Delete`/`Backspace`** carves the tick range out of every clip that *overlaps* it **on the marqueed tracks**, Ableton-style and **without rippling** — `SequencerCommand::RemoveClips` → `DeleteInRangeEdit::from_track_span` (`src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/delete_in_range.rs`). A clip fully inside the range is removed whole; a clip straddling one edge is trimmed to that edge keeping its id; a clip spanning the whole range becomes two (`[os, start]` keeps the id, `[end, oe]` is a new clip) with a gap between. Non-destructive, exactly like `⌘E` and the clip edge drag-resize: every piece keeps the *full* event list, only `start_tick`/`region` diverge (phase-locked), so dragging an edge back out afterward reveals the carved material again. Material after the range keeps its timeline position — the loop region and the cursor are untouched. Internally it composes two `SplitClipsEdit`s (cut every straddling clip at `end`, then at `start`, both scoped to the track span) plus a removal of every piece left fully inside the range. Selection sticks to the pre-delete clip if it survived, else clears. See `EditResult::RangeDeleted`/`RangeRestored` in `050-undo-redo.md`.
  - **`M` mutes** — Ableton-style ("Deactivate Time Selection"): `Some` bounds carve the tick range out of every clip **on the marqueed tracks** exactly like `Delete`/`Backspace` — split at `end`, then at `start` (`MuteInRangeEdit::from_track_span`, composing two `SplitClipsEdit`s the same way `DeleteInRangeEdit` does, same ordering and for the same reason) — but **mutes the interior instead of removing it**. There is no selected-clip fallback and no whole-clip toggle in `Clip`: like `Delete`, a clip is only ever muted through a tick range. Toggle rule: mute all if any interior clip is unmuted, otherwise unmute all. Non-destructive, like `⌘E`/`Delete`: every piece keeps the *full* event list, only `start_tick`/`region` diverge. Nothing is removed and nothing shifts, so there is no reselection. **The splits stay in place on an unmuting press**; undo reverses both the mute flags and the splits, LIFO. See `EditResult::RangeMuted`/`RangeUnmuted` in `050-undo-redo.md`.
  - **`M` mutes the selected *events*, not the clip, in `Clip`**: bare `Key::M` never reaches this Arranger-only forwarder outside the Arranger (there's no marquee there), so it falls through to `input_handler.rs`'s bare arm — `handle_mute` branches on `ClipContext` there: `ClipWithSelection` sends `SequencerCommand::MuteSelectedEvents` (`MuteSelectedEventsEdit`), toggling mute on the selected events (their `NoteOn` + paired `NoteOff` together) rather than the whole clip; every other context sends nothing; `Arranger` sends nothing (it can only get here with no marquee, and that press is already consumed upstream). It must never mute the *whole* clip out from under the events being edited. See `050-undo-redo.md`.
  - **`Q` quantizes in `Clip`, and only there**: bare `Key::Q` falls through to `input_handler.rs`, whose arm fires in `Clip` alone (`SequencerCommand::QuantizeEvents` → `QuantizeEventsEdit`): the selected notes, or the whole clip window with none selected. There is no `Q` in the Arranger — see `070-quantization.md`.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+E` splits clips at the cursor, Ableton-style**: bound in `Display::forward_input_event` and translated to `InputEvent::SplitClipsInTimeSelectionOrClip { time_bounds: self.gesture.time_selection }`. The whole `Option<TimeSelectionRect>` is forwarded as `SequencerCommand::SplitClips` and the sequencer handler resolves precedence: `Some` bounds split, at the cursor tick, every clip **on the marqueed tracks** that overlaps the tick range *and* whose bounds actually contain the cursor (`SplitClipsEdit::from_time_range_in_tracks`); `None` falls back to splitting just the selected clip (`SplitClipsEdit::from_selected_clip`), which no-ops when nothing is selected or the cursor sits at/outside the clip's bounds (a boundary split would produce an empty piece). **A zero-tick-width `Some` rect (a purely vertical marquee drag) is the deliberate way to split several tracks at one instant**: `find_clip_ids_in(tick, tick)` degenerates to "does this tick fall strictly inside a clip", which is exactly the containment `split_tick` needs anyway, so every clip under the point on the marqueed tracks gets split with no dedicated code path. Non-destructive, like the clip edge drag-resize in "Clip Edge Drag-Resize" below: both halves keep the *full* original event list and only `start_tick`/`region` diverge (phase-locked the same way left-edge trim is), so dragging an edge back out afterward can reveal the other half's content again — and, since no stored event is ever mutated to produce it, a note straddling the cut still plays through continuously across the boundary during transport (`Track::pending_note_offs`/`pending_note_ons`, `050-undo-redo.md`). The original clip keeps its id and becomes the left half; the right half is a new clip, so undoing a split removes it and restores the original's pre-split region end (`EditResult::ClipsSplit`/`ClipsUnsplit` in `050-undo-redo.md`). **The right-hand piece becomes the new selection** (`split_clips_workflow`, same cursor-clip reselection rule as `paste_clips_workflow`): the cursor sits exactly on the split boundary, i.e. at the start of the new right clip, so that's where the user's attention naturally lands next — mirrors Ableton. Undoing falls back to reselecting the restored original (left) clip rather than clearing the selection outright, since the usual `remove_clips_workflow` fallback (clear if the removed clip was selected) would otherwise leave nothing selected on every unsplit.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+I` inserts silence, Ableton/Bitwig-style, bypasses the marquee's track range, and the one binding with no single-clip fallback**: bound in `Display::forward_input_event` like `Delete`/`⌘E`, but since "how much time to insert" is meaningless without a real duration, the view only sends `InputEvent::InsertSilenceInSelection { start, end }` (extracting just the tick range from `self.gesture.time_selection`) when a selection is `Some`; with no selection the key press is simply consumed as a no-op, with no `Option`-wrapped payload and no precedence arm in `input_handler.rs` (unlike every other binding in this section). The insertion point is the selection's `start` (where the cursor already sits — see above) and the amount is `end - start`. `InsertSilenceEdit::from_time_range` (`src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/insert_silence.rs`) does the work in two steps, **always across every track, regardless of the marquee's track range** — this is deliberate, not a missed generalization: it first reuses `SplitClipsEdit::from_time_range` verbatim (constructing it with `split_tick` equal to the insertion point) to split any clip whose bounds straddle the insertion point, exactly as `⌘E` would; then it shifts every clip — pre-existing or freshly split — whose `start_tick` is at or past the insertion point forward by the inserted amount, via a raw `Clip::set_start_tick` mutation rather than a `remove_clip_by_id`/`add_clip` round-trip (safe because a uniform forward shift of an already-non-overlapping clip set can never create a new overlap, and `Track::add_clip`'s overlap check has no awareness of sibling clips mid-shift in the same edit, so re-validating one at a time would risk spurious failure). Scoping this to fewer than every track would slide those tracks out of sync with the rest — the same reasoning that keeps `Shift+⌘D` (Duplicate Time) global on both halves, see below. The loop region and the cursor are **not** shifted — same "intentional-only" scope as every other key in this section. No reselection happens either: a shifted or split-left clip keeps its id, so whatever was already selected stays valid and simply tracks its new position via `ClipUpdated`. See `EditResult::SilenceInserted`/`SilenceRemoved` in `050-undo-redo.md`.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+Delete` deletes time, the exact opposite of `⌘/Ctrl+I` — bypasses the marquee's track range, same no-single-clip-fallback shape**: bound in `Display::forward_input_event` ahead of the plain `Delete`/`Backspace` arm (which has no modifier guard and would otherwise catch this chord first), matched only on `Key::Delete` (not `Backspace` — there is no `⌘Backspace` binding). Like `⌘/Ctrl+I`, only `modifiers.command` is checked — `Shift` is not read, so `⌘+Shift+Delete` fires the same binding rather than falling through to plain delete. This deliberately does **not** mirror Ableton Live, which binds Delete Time to `⌘/Ctrl+Shift+Delete`: that extra `Shift` groups it with Ableton's other track-range-bypassing chords, but `⌘/Ctrl+I` itself is already a single-modifier bypass with no marquee-scoped sibling to distinguish from, so `⌘/Ctrl+Delete` pairs with it the same way instead — the plain chord was free precisely because there is no marquee-scoped Delete Time to reserve it for. The view only sends `InputEvent::DeleteTimeInSelection { start, end }` when a selection is `Some`; with no selection the key press is consumed as a no-op. `DeleteTimeEdit::from_time_range` (`src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/delete_time.rs`) does the work in two steps, **always across every track**, for the same alignment reason as `⌘/Ctrl+I`: it first reuses `DeleteInRangeEdit::from_time_range` verbatim (the exact same non-rippling carve `Delete`/`Backspace` uses) to clear `[start, end)` — trimming or splitting every overlapping clip, removing anything left fully inside the range; then it shifts every clip — pre-existing or freshly carved — whose `start_tick` is at or past `end` **back** by `end - start`, via the same raw `Clip::set_start_tick` mutation `⌘/Ctrl+I` uses (safe for the same non-overlap reason, mirrored: the carve already guarantees `[start, end)` is empty, so a uniform backward shift of the already-non-overlapping remainder can't create a new overlap). The loop region and the cursor are **not** shifted — same "intentional-only" scope as `⌘/Ctrl+I`. No reselection on the shift half either, for the same reason as `⌘/Ctrl+I`; but since the carve half *can* remove the selected clip outright (unlike `⌘/Ctrl+I`, which never removes anything), the clip selection falls back to clearing when that happens — the same fallback `Delete`/`Backspace` uses. Unlike `⌘/Ctrl+I` (which leaves the marquee untouched — the selection still frames real content, just shifted), the Arranger *time selection* (the marquee itself, distinct from clip selection above) no longer refers to anything real once its span is deleted, so the handler collapses it back to the track cursor: `handle_edit_result` sends `UiEvent::TimeSelectionCleared` after a `TimeDeleted` result, which `ui_events.rs` turns into `clear_time_selection()`. Undo restores it: `EditResult::TimeUndeleted` carries `restored_selection: (i32, i32)` (the edit's frozen `start`/`end`, not re-derived from view state — same "frozen at construction" rule as everything else in `050-undo-redo.md`), and the handler re-sends it as `UiEvent::TimeSelectionSet { start, end }` — the same event `⌘/Ctrl+D` uses to advance the selection, just restoring instead of advancing. See `EditResult::TimeDeleted`/`TimeUndeleted` in `050-undo-redo.md`.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+D` — the two Duplicate bindings, one key, two different operations**: `Display::forward_input_event` matches `Key::D` with `modifiers.command`, branching on `modifiers.shift`. Plain `⌘/Ctrl+D` is **"Duplicate Clips"** (marquee-scoped, overwriting — the everyday one); `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+D` is Ableton-style **"Duplicate Time"** (global, space-inserting — the heavier one, per the ⇧-means-wider convention `⌘C`/`Shift+⌘C` set). There is no bare-`D` Arranger binding at all — these two cover every duplicate need. Both forms are selection-only — nothing is sent without a real time selection — and both *move* the time selection onto the copy afterward via `UiEvent::TimeSelectionSet` (the cursor is not moved, so `sync_time_selection_to_cursor` leaves the pushed selection alone), so repeated presses chain copies down the timeline.
  - **Plain ⌘/Ctrl+D — "Duplicate Clips" (marquee-scoped, overwriting)**: sends `InputEvent::DuplicateClipsInSelection { rect: TimeSelectionRect }` → `SequencerCommand::DuplicateClips` → `DuplicateClipsEdit::from_track_span` (`src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/duplicate_clips.rs`) → `SequencerEdit::DuplicateClips`. It is plain `⌘C` then `⌘V` at the selection's `end`, composed: a thin anchor-picking newtype over `PasteClipsEdit`, built from a throwaway `Sequencer::copy_range_to_clipboard((track_start, track_end), start, end)` (the same pieces plain `⌘C` produces) pasted via `from_clipboard_at(.., end)`, so each piece lands at `original + width` on its own track. **Nothing shifts and no time is inserted**: the copy is dropped on top of whatever already sits in `[end, 2*end - start)` on the marqueed tracks, and that material is carved out by `PasteClipsEdit`'s per-target `DeleteInRangeEdit` — the copy wins. Tracks outside the marquee are untouched, and the loop region is never moved. Reports `EditResult::ClipsDuplicated`/`ClipsUnduplicated` — `ClipsPasted`/`ClipsUnpasted`'s buckets plus `new_selection`/`restored_selection: TimeSelectionRect` — and the handler runs `paste_clips_workflow`/`unpaste_clips_workflow` verbatim then emits `TimeSelectionSet { .., track_span: Some(..) }`: the edit froze its own rect, so the advanced marquee names its track span in full.
  - **Shift+⌘/Ctrl+D — "Duplicate Time" (global, space-inserting)**: sends `InputEvent::DuplicateTimeInSelection { start, end }` (tick range only — bypasses the marquee's track range) → `SequencerCommand::DuplicateTimeInRange` → `DuplicateTimeEdit::from_time_range` (`src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/duplicate_time.rs`). It is `⌘/Ctrl+I` followed by a paste, composed: it first runs an `InsertSilenceEdit` anchored at the selection's `end` (insert `end - start` ticks — split any straddler, shift every clip at/after `end` right, on every track), then a `PasteClipsEdit` that drops a copy of the `[start, end)` slice of every overlapping clip on every track into the freed span. Nothing is carved/destroyed, because the gap is pre-cleared. It is deliberately global on *both* halves — a variant that shifted every track but pasted only some would be the worst of both worlds; the alignment argument that keeps `⌘/Ctrl+I` global (a partial shift desyncs the marqueed tracks from the rest) applies to the shift half, and once the shift is global there is no point scoping the paste. Afterwards the handler emits `UiEvent::TimeSelectionSet { start, end, track_span: None }` to advance the tick range to `[end, 2*end - start)`. Since `DuplicateTimeEdit` carries no track-span state (only `start`/`end`), `UiEvent::TimeSelectionSet`'s handler (`ui_events.rs`) preserves whatever track range was already active in `Display.gesture.time_selection`, defaulting to `self.selected_track_idx` only if there was no prior selection to preserve from (the clip band press and plain `⌘D` are the senders that name their span — `track_span: Some(..)` — see "Clip Band Press & Move Drag" below). The **loop region is the exception to the "intentional-only" scope** every other key in this section keeps: if its start sits within the duplicated span or after it (`region_start >= start`) it is *moved* (not resized) right by the selection width — `DuplicateTimeEdit` calls `Sequencer::shift_region`, and `undo()` reverses it — so a loop stays on the same musical material after the insert. A region starting before the selection is left alone (moving only its end would be a resize). See `EditResult::TimeDuplicated`/`TimeUnduplicated` in `050-undo-redo.md`.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+J` merges clips — Ableton's Consolidate, marquee-only**: bound in `Display::forward_input_event` (Arranger, exact chord — Shift or ⌥ refuses) like plain `⌘/Ctrl+D`; with no selection nothing is sent. `InputEvent::MergeClipsInSelection { rect }` → `SequencerCommand::MergeClips` → `PasteClipsEdit::merging` (`src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/merge_clips.rs`) — a paste of the merged clips, like `⌘/Ctrl+D`. **One merged clip per marqueed track, never across tracks**, spanning exactly the marquee's tick range: gaps between clips become silence, a clip sticking out of the range keeps its outside part as its own clip (the `Delete` carve), and a marqueed track with no clip in the range is left alone. **The merge sounds exactly as before** (`Clip::merged`, `models/clip/merge.rs`): per clip, a note whose `NoteOn` is inside the clip's window comes over with its end stopped at the window end (playback releases it there, so a note cut by a split stays cut rather than being healed), other events inside the window come over as they are, and hidden material outside a window is dropped — the one destructive part, which undo restores. Notes of a muted clip come over as muted notes; the merged clip is unmuted, its window starts at 0, its swing is the earliest clip's, and its events get fresh ids. With one clip in the range it bakes that clip: a crop when it has hidden material, padding when the marquee is wider, a three-way split with the middle baked when narrower — and **nothing at all** (no undo step) when the clip spans exactly the range with nothing hidden. Afterwards the marquee is untouched and covers exactly the merged clip(s), and the merged clip on the selected track becomes the lead (so the clip panel shows it), wherever the cursor sits; undo restores the clips and the selection. Use it to export several clips as one `.mid` (`240` § A2).
- **`Shift+⌘/Ctrl+C` copies, `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+X` cuts the time selection across every track (bypasses the marquee), `⌘/Ctrl+V` pastes**: `egui-winit` turns the copy/cut/paste chords into `egui::Event::Copy` / `egui::Event::Cut` / `egui::Event::Paste` and **swallows the raw `C`/`X`/`V` key presses**, regardless of Shift, so `InputPoller` maps those to `InputEvent::Copy { shift }` / `InputEvent::Cut { shift }` / `InputEvent::Paste` (bare `C` with no modifier is unaffected and unbound). Since the bare `egui::Event::Copy`/`Cut` carries no modifier payload of its own, `InputPoller` samples `shift` separately from `ctx.input(|i| i.modifiers)` in the same frame — safe because `egui-winit` updates that from `WindowEvent::ModifiersChanged` ahead of dispatching the `C`/`X` keydown. `Display::forward_input_event` resolves `InputEvent::Copy { shift: true }`/`InputEvent::Cut { shift: true }` (Arranger only) into `InputEvent::CopyClipsInSelection`/`CutClipsInSelection { start, end }` — extracting just the tick range, since these two always span every track regardless of the marquee — and **only when `self.gesture.time_selection` is `Some`**: every clipboard chord is marquee-only, the same no-single-clip-fallback shape as `Delete`/`Backspace`, so with no time selection the chord is consumed and nothing is sent. **Plain `⌘/Ctrl+C`/`⌘/Ctrl+X` (`shift: false`) forward the whole `TimeSelectionRect` to the marquee-scoped siblings** — see the bullet below. `InputEvent::Paste` needs no view-local operand and is dispatched straight to `SequencerCommand::PasteClips` in `input_handler.rs`, Arranger-gated. **`egui::Event::Paste` only fires when the OS text clipboard is non-empty**, so a copy/cut also writes `input_event::CLIP_CLIPBOARD_SENTINEL` to it via `EventHandlers.repaint_ctx` (`prime_os_clipboard_for_clips`: `ctx.copy_text` + `request_repaint`, the same cross-thread wake pattern as `110-performance-lane.md`); the pasted text itself is never read.
  - **Copy** (`SequencerCommand::CopyClips { start, end }`, `Sequencer::copy_clips_to_clipboard` in `src/core/sequencer/clipboard.rs`) is **not** an undoable edit — copying is not a data mutation, exactly like a selection. It copies the portion of every clip on every track that intersects `[start, end)` as a non-destructive region-windowed piece (phase-locked, full event list retained — identical to `⌘E`/`Delete`), each piece's `start_tick` normalized to the selection start and each piece tagged with its absolute source track. There is no whole-clip fallback: a band press already marquees exactly the clip's span, so "copy this one clip" is the ordinary range copy. The `ClipClipboard` is session-only in-memory state, not persisted, cleared on new/load project (`Sequencer::new_project`).
  - **Cut** (`SequencerCommand::CutClips { start, end }`) is copy-then-delete: it calls `Sequencer::copy_clips_to_clipboard(start, end)` (non-undoable clipboard fill), then runs `DeleteInRangeEdit::from_time_range` (the same Ableton-style range carve-out `Delete`/`Backspace` use, but across every track, no rippling). Marquee-only like copy: the view only sends `InputEvent::CutClipsInSelection { start, end }` when a selection is `Some`. Only the delete goes on the undo record, so undoing a cut restores the removed clips while the clipboard keeps the cut payload — mirrors Ableton. No dedicated `EditResult`/`SequencerEdit` variant: it reuses `RangeDeleted`/`RangeRestored`.
  - **Paste** (`SequencerCommand::PasteClips` → `PasteClipsEdit`, `src/core/sequencer/edit/clip_edits/paste.rs`) re-anchors every clipboard piece to the cursor tick and, in the track dimension, follows the clipboard's **track anchor** (`ClipClipboard::track_anchor`, resolved per piece by `ClipClipboard::target_track`): the all-track `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+C`/`X` forms have none, so their pieces go back on their **absolute source track** whatever is selected (the same alignment argument that makes them bypass the marquee — re-anchoring an every-track copy would just slide the whole song down), and a paste of one needs no track selection at all. The marquee-scoped plain `⌘/Ctrl+C`/`X` forms anchor on the marquee's `track_start`, so `⌘/Ctrl+V` lands the set on the **selected track with the marquee's shape** — tracks 3..=5 copied and track 2 selected pastes onto 2..=4, empty marqueed tracks included in the shape (the offset is from `track_start`, not from the first piece); pieces that would land past the last track are dropped, and with no track selected the scoped clipboard falls back to the absolute source tracks too. Both Duplicate edits pass `target_track: None` to `from_clipboard_at`, so their throwaway copies always stay on their source tracks. Where a pasted clip overlaps existing material, that span is carved out Ableton-style by composing one **track-scoped** `DeleteInRangeEdit::from_track_range` per pasted clip (the same worked-example reuse pattern as `InsertSilenceEdit`/`DeleteInRangeEdit` composing `SplitClipsEdit`). The cursor and loop region are untouched — same "intentional-only" scope. The pasted clip under the cursor on the selected track becomes the selection (same rule as `split_clips_workflow`). Plain `⌘D` (Duplicate Clips) and `Shift+⌘D` (Duplicate Time) both reuse this same paste primitive with a selection-derived anchor instead of the cursor — see above. See `EditResult::ClipsPasted`/`ClipsUnpasted` in `050-undo-redo.md`.
- **Plain `⌘/Ctrl+C` copies, plain `⌘/Ctrl+X` cuts — every clip within the marquee rectangle**: the marquee-scoped siblings of `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+C`/`X` above. `Display::forward_input_event` resolves `InputEvent::Copy { shift: false }`/`Cut { shift: false }` into `InputEvent::CopyClipsInSelectionScoped`/`CutClipsInSelectionScoped { rect }` → `SequencerCommand::CopyClipsScoped`/`CutClipsScoped { rect }` — forwarding the whole `TimeSelectionRect`, and only when a selection is `Some` (marquee-only, like `Delete`/`Backspace` and the `Shift+⌘` forms — with no selection the chord is consumed and nothing is copied or deleted). A rect acts on every clip **on tracks `rect.track_start..=rect.track_end`** intersecting `[rect.start, rect.end)` — exactly the marquee rectangle, the same scope `Delete`/`M`/`⌘E` use. A rect that overlaps nothing is a no-op (clipboard untouched / nothing deleted) — there is no whole-clip fallback of any kind.
  - **Copy** (`Sequencer::copy_clips_scoped_to_clipboard`, `src/core/sequencer/clipboard.rs`) delegates straight to `copy_range_to_clipboard((rect.track_start, rect.track_end), rect.start, rect.end)` — the exact same track-range-filtered function `Shift+⌘C` uses for the all-track case, just narrower — producing a multi-piece, absolute-track-indexed clipboard — then stamps `track_anchor = Some(rect.track_start)` on it, which is what lets `⌘/Ctrl+V` re-anchor the set onto the selected track (see Paste above).
  - **Cut** (`Sequencer::cut_clips_scoped_workflow`, `src/core/event_handlers/clip_range_edits.rs`) is copy-then-delete like `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+X`, and reuses the **same** edit type Delete/Backspace use — `DeleteInRangeEdit::from_track_span(sequencer, rect.track_start, rect.track_end, rect.start, rect.end)` — rather than a dedicated clip-scoped edit type. There is no `None` form: like every delete, it's marquee-only. See `050-undo-redo.md`.
  - **Visual**: every clip renders the same flat tint in its own track colour — **the lead/cursor-selected clip gets no visual at all** (no tint bump, no brighter header, no `ClipSelected` UI event): a highlighted clip would promise that Delete/Duplicate/Cut act on it, and they never do — the marquee is the only thing they read. A clip untouched by the marquee must look exactly as if there were no selection at all. `draw_clip_body` (`src/view/display/rendering/arranger.rs`) then paints one *additional* highlight on top, only when `time_selection` is active **and has a real tick width** (`TimeSelectionRect::has_tick_range` — a zero-tick-width point-in-time track selection, see above, has no range to visualize): the sub-rect of the clip that actually overlaps `[rect.start, rect.end)` **and** whose track falls inside `rect.track_start..=rect.track_end` (`tick_overlap`, `rendering/mod.rs`) gets a neutral `theme::fg_dim()` highlight, in place of the track colour, so a marquee spanning several tracks reads as one unified selection rather than a patchwork of per-track tints. Nothing outside that sub-rect changes — on this clip or any other. Do not also dim the rest of the arranger while a marquee is active: it reads as the whole view flashing gray on every drag. Empty gaps within the tick range on the marqueed track lanes only (not elsewhere) get the same highlight at full row height (`draw_time_selection_gaps`/`gap_spans`, iterating `rect.track_start..=rect.track_end` instead of every track) — the highlight marks what's inside the marquee (what plain `⌘/Ctrl+C/X` would grab; `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+C/X` always grabs every track regardless of what's tinted). The clip header strip is unaffected by `time_selection` and marks nothing — it's a track identifier and a grab band. See `030-ui-design.md`.

## General Layering Principle

- **Input handlers express intent only**: key/mouse routing in `event_handlers/input_handler.rs` should dispatch commands and avoid owning state-entry defaults or business side effects.
- **Transition workflows own state-entry/exit side effects**: defaults that belong to entering/leaving a mode (for example, clearing the event selection on leaving `Clip`) should live in `workflows.rs` / sequencer command handling where the transition is actually committed.
- **Model/sequencer layers own mutation and invariants**: data edits, normalization, and consistency rules should stay in `src/models/` and `src/core/sequencer/`, not in input dispatch code.

## Tracks and Regions

- **1 to `MAX_TRACKS` (16) tracks**, four in a new project; the count is the length of `Sequencer::tracks` (in the view, `Display::track_count()`). `MAX_TRACKS` only sizes the per-track arrays — never loop to it where the live count is meant.
- **Adding and removing tracks** (`050` § Track add / remove): ⌘T adds an empty MIDI track after the selected one, the `+` row under the last lane (`030`) one at the end; `Delete`/`Backspace` with the track headers focused (§ Views, Track header focus) removes the selected track, the output menu's **Delete track** its own. Both undoable, both refused with a footer message at the limits (16 tracks / the last track) and during a live take. **Renaming** (⌘R, a double-click on the name, the menu's **Rename** — § Track Rename) is undoable too, and allowed during a take (it moves nothing).
  - **Selection after**: an added (or undo-restored) track is selected. Removing the selected track selects the one that took its place — the one above when it was the last; removing another track leaves the selection on its track. A remove of the selected track also leaves the clip view if it was open.
  - **Positional view state follows the shift**: every track below an insert / remove changes position, so `TracksChanged` carries `shift: Option<TrackShift>` (`models/track.rs` — `Inserted(at)` / `Removed(at)`, `apply` / `apply_span`, unit-tested) and `Display::follow_track_shift` renumbers the clip shapes (a removed track's go), drops any drag in flight and the output menu, and moves the selected track to where the sequencer puts it (the added track; after a remove the same track, or the one that took the removed selected track's place) — re-latching the marquee's collapse watcher, so the edit's own selection move never reads as the user's. The marquee is **cleared on a remove** and **kept on an add**, its track span shifted (widened if the new track lands inside it). Engine-side state needs nothing — it is keyed by slot.
- **No hard length constraints** on tracks, clips, or regions.
- **Clips on a track must not overlap**: `Track::add_clip` enforces a non-overlap invariant and returns `bool` — callers must handle `false`. Every insertion path goes through `add_clip`.
- **`Region`** (see `src/models/region.rs`) defines the cycle length for both clips and the arrangement. It stores `start`/`end` as `Arc<AtomicI32>` so the UI can read them without locking.
- **Region does not auto-follow cursor in Arranger**: moving the cursor with `L/R` (or jumping it edge to edge with `⌥←`/`⌥→`) does not shift the region window. Region changes are intentional-only — via `⌘/Ctrl+L` (snap to the time selection; with no selection it toggles looping in place instead), the *only* keyboard-driven region mutation left. Note that a time selection itself never moves the region — only `⌘/Ctrl+L` commits it. `SHIFT+UP`/`SHIFT+DOWN` extend the time selection's track span, not the region.
- **Playback start follows cursor exactly**: `PlayFromCursor` / restart-from-cursor must honor `cursor_tick` even when cursor is outside the current region window. Region loop wrap applies when playback is inside the region; do not snap start to `region.start` just because cursor is past `region.end`.
- **Plain `+/-` and `Shift+/-` zoom in `Arranger` (and in `Clip`), never touch the region** (the zoom is view-local, `010-keybindings.md`): the loop region has no keyboard shortcut — only `⌘/Ctrl+L` mutates it — and a clip's region length is changed by dragging the clip header band's edge or by `[`/`]` (see "Clip Edge Drag-Resize" below).
- **`Delete` never touches the region**: it keys off the time selection only (see "Arranger Time Selection" above), not the loop region or the selected clip, and carves partial clips (`DeleteInRangeEdit`) rather than acting on whole clips only. It is acceptable for clips or events to exceed region bounds when contextually sensible.

## Event Selection

- **No reserved insert spans**: a capture commit just adds events, and never changes the selection (see Views above).
- **Only notes are selectable, so every note edit leaves recorded wheel moves alone**: a clip can hold pitch-bend and mod-wheel events (`090`), but selection (click, marquee, select-all) only ever holds `NoteOn`s, the piano roll and thumbnails draw only notes, and `Clip::reach` (how far the clip cursor goes) counts only note edges. So move, resize, transpose, nudge, delete, duplicate, copy/paste and quantize (`070`) never touch them — a moved note's bend stays where it was played. No lane shows or edits them (`240` § D).
- **Event selection is click-based, not keyboard-navigated**: clicking a note in the piano roll selects it (`SequencerCommand::SelectClipEvent`, resolved via a hit-test in `Display::event_id_at` against `self.render.event_shapes`, using the same geometry — `Display::note_area_geom` — as `draw_clip_view` so the clickable area always matches what's drawn). A click always fully replaces the selection: `select_clip_event_workflow` clears first, then selects, so clicking away from a multi-selection correctly deselects every member, not just the previous lead. `⌘/Ctrl+A` selects all `NoteOn` events (`handle_select_all` in `input_handler.rs`, from `Clip`, publishing the selection) and ESC is the only key that clears it; `Shift+L/R` never touches the selection (`010-keybindings.md`). `Up/Down` transposes pitch; arrow keys never navigate events.
- **Deselecting all events is "click empty grid" or ESC**: an empty-grid click resolves to `SelectClipEvent(None)`, which the workflow treats identically to a real deselect. ESC (`ClearEventSelection`) is a no-op with nothing selected.

### Note Insert (double-click)

- **Clip pane only, empty grid only**: `InputPoller` emits `InputEvent::MouseDoubleClicked { x, y }` on the **second press** — its own `DoubleClick` detector (a click that didn't drag, then a press within egui's `InputOptions::max_double_click_delay` and `max_click_dist` of where it ended, read from the context each frame; a quick third press starts over), not egui's `button_double_clicked`, which only fires on the second release and made the note appear when the button came up. It follows that press's own `MouseClicked`, so both presses have already done their ordinary work (deselect, clip cursor); the event marquee the second press started is dropped when a note is inserted, so holding and moving afterwards can't marquee the new note's selection away. `forward_input_event` routes it to `Display::handle_double_click` when the pointer is over the clip pane; it does nothing unless the view is `Clip`, the pointer is over the note grid (`is_mouse_inside_grid` plus `note_row_at_screen_y` — not the key column, timeline strip or velocity panel) and `event_id_at` finds no note there.
- **What it sends**: `InputEvent::InsertNote { tick, length, pitch, velocity }`, `tick` = `snapped_tick_at` — the nearest visible grid line, exactly where the hover cursor line is drawn, so the note lands on the line the user sees, `length`/`velocity` = the last-used pair, `Display.gesture.last_note` — set by a note resize drag (below) to the pressed note's new length and its velocity; view-local, kept across view visits, not persisted; until set, `PPQN` (a beat) at `Display::DRAWN_NOTE_VELOCITY` (100). The sequencer side is `InsertNotesEdit` (`050-undo-redo.md`): clamped into the clip window, same-pitch overlaps trimmed, the new note selected — a deliberate exception to "a commit never changes the selection", which is a capture rule — and auditioned.

### Note Move & Resize Drag

- **Clip pane only, a plain press on a note, decided at press time**: after the ⌘/Ctrl velocity-drag check, `MouseClicked` hit-tests with `Display::note_hit_at` — `event_id_at` plus which part of the note is under `x` (`note_part_at`, pure, against the note's drawn x span): an edge zone of `NOTE_EDGE_HIT_PX` (5px) inside either end, capped at a third of the note's width so a tiny note keeps a body, else the body (`NotePart::Start`/`Body`/`End`). The zones never reach outside the note, so the grid beside it stays empty grid. On a hit `Display::begin_note_drag` takes over: no event marquee starts — dragging from a note moves it — and a press anywhere else keeps the click/marquee behaviour above.
- **Targets**: the velocity drag's rule — the whole selection when the pressed note is in it, else the pressed note alone. A press on an *unselected* note is sent at once as the ordinary click (`MouseClickedTicks`: select it alone, audition, clip cursor to the snapped tick). A press on a *selected* note sends nothing, so the group stays selected to move; if the press never becomes a drag, release sends that held-back click (`NoteMouseDrag::deferred_click_tick`) — the arranger marquee band press's shape.
- **Applied live, one undo step per gesture**: `Display.gesture.note_drag: Option<NoteMouseDrag>` (`pointer_state.rs`) records the targets with their spans at the press (`origin`) and a `drag_id` (`GestureState::take_drag_id`), and latches `dragging` past `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX`; `extend_note_drag` then recomputes a `NoteDrag` from the press each `MouseMoved` — the time delta is the pointer's tick offset from the press rounded to whole visible grid steps (`snap_to_grid` on the offset, which rounds negative values like positive ones — *relative*: a recorded note keeps its off-grid feel and a pure pitch move never shifts it in time; `Q` puts notes on the grid), the move's pitch delta the row under the pointer (`note_at_screen_y`, clamped to the visible range) minus the press row. The body moves (`NoteDrag::Move`), the left edge resizes the start keeping the end (`ResizeStart`), the right edge the end (`ResizeEnd`); the kind is fixed at press and kept by `NoteDrag::with_deltas`. Each change sends `InputEvent::DragNotes { event_ids, drag, drag_id }` with the whole drag so far, so the clip — and playback — follow the pointer; the sequencer applies each step to the notes as the gesture found them and merges the gesture's steps into one undo step (`DragNotesEdit`, `050-undo-redo.md`). A move that stays in the same grid step and row sends nothing. `draw_clip_view` paints each dragged note where `Display::note_drag_preview` puts its `origin` span — `NoteDrag::dragged_spans`, the model's own clamping rule — in the selection accent, and only there, so it tracks the pointer without waiting for the round-trip; it reads `origin`, not the shapes, because the shapes follow the model as each step lands. Notes a step trims (same-pitch overlap) show through the shapes. On release (`finish_note_drag`) a resize sets the last-used length/velocity, and the dragged shapes are moved to their landing spans in place (`EventShape::set_span`) in case the last step's `EventsUpdated` is still on its way — without it the notes could flash back for those frames. Targets come from `Display::drag_targets`, shared with the velocity drag. Esc mid-drag (`cancel_note_drag`) sends the drag back to nothing, which puts the notes back and drops the gesture's undo step; the drag is also cleared with the other gestures on clip entry/exit and project load (what was applied stays).
- **Past the edge**: a move held with the pointer past the note area's top or bottom auto-scrolls the view and the pitch follows the row at the edge (`Display::auto_scroll_note_area`, `030` § Piano Roll); a resize has no pitch and doesn't scroll.
- **Audition on each pitch change**: whenever the pressed note's previewed pitch changes, `extend_note_drag` sends `InputEvent::PreviewNote { note, velocity }` → `SequencerCommand::PreviewNote` → `Sequencer::preview_pitch` (the selected track's channel, through `preview_note`, so plugin tracks hear it too).
- **During playback**: each step that takes a sounding note away from the playhead (moved away, new pitch, end or start past it) releases it, and a note stretched step by step keeps sounding to its new end — `Track::edit_clip_events`, shared by every event edit (`050-undo-redo.md`).
- **Cursor**: `Display.gesture.note_hover` (`update_note_hover`, clip pane, no button or ⌘/Ctrl held) shows `ResizeHorizontal` over an edge; a resize drag keeps it, a move drag shows `Grabbing` once latched.

### Event Marquee Selection

- **Clip only, same press-drag-release shape as the Arranger's time selection** (see above), but with one key difference: a time selection is purely view-local, while event selection is model state (`Clip::event_selection`) because it drives real edits (nudge/delete/duplicate/transpose) and the key routing (`has_event_selection`). So the drag rectangle has to round-trip through a command on every move rather than staying entirely in `Display`.
- **View-local drag state**: `Display.gesture.event_marquee_anchor: Option<MarqueeAnchor>` (the press point in screen space for the drag threshold, and in content space — tick and note row units — for the box; `Some` only while the primary button is held — doubles as the drag-active flag) and `Display.gesture.event_marquee_rect: Option<(i32, i32, f32, f32)>` (normalized `(tick_min, tick_max, row_min, row_max)` where the rows are content-space row units (`NoteAreaGeom::row_at`), not snapped to note rows, `Some` only once the pointer has moved off the anchor). Content space keeps the anchor on its notes if the user wheel-scrolls under a held marquee; the pointer's end is held to the visible rows. The marquee never edge-auto-scrolls (`030` § Piano Roll): the view sliding under a sweep is distracting while its notes audition. The rect is for rendering only — `Clip::event_selection` is the source of truth for which events are actually selected.
- **Every rect update round-trips through the model**: `Display::extend_event_marquee` (driven by `MouseMoved`, alongside `extend_time_selection`) recomputes the rect and sends `InputEvent::SelectEventsInRect { tick_min, tick_max, note_min, note_max }`, which `input_handler.rs` maps 1:1 to `SequencerCommand::SelectEventsInRect`. `select_events_in_rect_workflow` calls `Clip::select_events_in_rect`, which recomputes the whole matching set from scratch each time (rather than incrementally adding/removing), so a shrinking rectangle naturally deselects events that fall back outside it. The workflow emits `EventSelected`/`EventDeselected` UI events only for the diff, then calls `publish_event_selection` — one of the call sites listed under Views above.
- **Overlap selects, not full containment**: a `NoteOn` event is included if its span overlaps `[tick_min, tick_max)` at all — matches Ableton/Bitwig/Logic, not a literal "fully surrounded" reading.
- **No grid snap**: unlike cursor placement, the rectangle bounds are unsnapped screen-derived ticks/notes — it's a hit-test box, not a musical position.
- **A small drag threshold gates the first update**: a plain click almost always produces a sub-pixel `MouseMoved` even with no intentional drag, and the rectangle's overlap test uses different boundary semantics than `event_id_at`'s click hit-test, so sending a rect update on that jitter could immediately clobber the click's own `SelectClipEvent` selection. The drag latches once the pointer has moved `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX` from the anchor (`past_drag_threshold`) — `event_marquee_rect` turning `Some` is the latch; before that, `extend_event_marquee` is a no-op. Once latched it stays latched for the rest of the drag, so coming back near the anchor afterward keeps updating normally rather than freezing.
- **Auditions only the notes that just joined**: like `SelectClipEvent`, the marquee auditions, but only the update's diff — `select_events_in_rect_workflow` plays the `newly selected` ids together through `Sequencer::preview_notes` (one note-off thread per update on a `MidiOut` track), picked by `Sequencer::marquee_audition_note_ons` (pure `Clip::audition_note_ons`): earliest first (lowest pitch on a tie), one per pitch, at most `MARQUEE_AUDITION_MAX_NOTES` (4) per update. Sweeping across a melody plays it note by note; an unchanged rect (most `MouseMoved`s) is silent; a sweep that swallows a dense passage in one update sounds a handful, not every note; a note that leaves and rejoins the rect sounds again. Nothing plays on release — the drag already played what joined.
- **Release drops the drag state, not the selection**: `MouseReleased` clears `event_marquee_anchor`/`event_marquee_rect` (`Display::clear_event_marquee`) but leaves whatever got selected in place, exactly like releasing a mouse button ends a drag without undoing its effect. Both fields are also cleared on `ClipEntered`/`ClipExited`/`ProjectLoaded`, alongside `clear_time_selection`, so no stale box survives a view change.

### Velocity Drag

- **Clip only, ⌘/Ctrl+press-drag on a note, decided at press time**: `MouseClicked` checks the modifier and hit-tests with `Display::event_id_at` *before* the normal cursor-placement/marquee logic runs; if both hit, `Display::begin_velocity_drag` takes over and the click is *not* forwarded as `MouseClickedTicks` — this is a distinct gesture, not a modified click. There is no separate "hovered event" concept; the press location doubles as the hover check, same as a marquee's press doubles as its anchor.
- **Target resolution matches Ableton**: if the pressed note is part of the current selection (read from `Display.render.event_shapes`' cached `is_selected()`, the same source `event_id_at` hit-tests against), the whole selection's velocities move together; otherwise only the pressed note is affected and the real selection (`Clip::event_selection`) is left untouched — this is why the underlying edit can't use the `clip_events_edit!` macro (see `DragEventsVelocityEdit` in `event_edits.rs`).
- **View-local drag state**: `Display.gesture.velocity_drag: Option<VelocityDrag>` (anchor y, drag id, target event ids, running total nudge — `Some` only while held, doubling as the drag-active flag) and `Display.gesture.next_velocity_drag_id`, a counter handed out once per gesture. Only vertical movement matters; `x` is ignored.
- **Every nudge round-trips through the model, like the marquee**: `Display::extend_velocity_drag` (driven by `MouseMoved`) recomputes the total nudge from the anchor (`VELOCITY_DRAG_PX_PER_UNIT`), diffs it against the last value sent, and — only when nonzero — sends `InputEvent::DragEventsVelocity { event_ids, nudge, drag_id }`, mapped 1:1 to `SequencerCommand::DragEventsVelocity`.
- **Unlike the marquee, this *is* a data mutation, so it must still undo as one step**: continuous `MouseMoved`-driven velocity nudges are coalesced by `undo::Edit::merge` on `SequencerEdit` (see `050-undo-redo.md`), gated by `drag_id` so two separate drags on the same notes never merge into one undo entry.
- **Release just drops the drag state**: `MouseReleased` clears `velocity_drag`; whatever velocity change happened stays, exactly like the marquee's selection persists after its drag state clears.

### Note Copy / Cut / Paste

- **Clip only, the clip's own clipboard**: plain `⌘/Ctrl+C` / `⌘/Ctrl+X` / `⌘/Ctrl+V` arrive as `InputEvent::Copy { shift: false }` / `Cut { shift: false }` / `Paste` (same egui-winit story as the arranger's, above). `Display::forward_input_event` only resolves them in `Arranger`, so in `Clip` they fall through to `input_handler.rs` → `SequencerCommand::CopyNotes` / `CutNotes` / `PasteNotes`. The `⇧` forms do nothing in `Clip`. The note clipboard (`Sequencer::note_clipboard`, `sequencer/clipboard.rs`) is separate from the clip clipboard, so a note copy never discards a clip copy and each view pastes its own kind. Session-only, cleared on new/load project. Both copies prime the OS clipboard with the sentinel so `⌘V` fires (`prime_os_clipboard`).
- **Copy** (`Clip::copy_selected_notes`) takes the selected notes (pitch, velocity, mute, length), each placed relative to the earliest selected note's start. Not undoable. With nothing selected it is a no-op that keeps the clipboard (no whole-clip fallback; `⌘/Ctrl+A` first copies everything). **Cut** is copy then `DeleteSelectedEventsEdit` — only the delete is an undo step.
- **Paste** (`InsertNotesEdit`, undoable) lands the earliest copied note exactly on the **clip cursor** — where the user last clicked or stepped — and the rest keep their spacing after it; pitches unchanged. It works into any clip (copy in one, open another, paste). Only notes that start inside the clip window are pasted, and their ends stop at the window end, the mouse edits' rule (a paste never extends the clip or hides notes outside it). Same-pitch overlaps are resolved like an insert (`Clip::insert_notes`). The pasted notes become the selection — like `⌘/Ctrl+D` and the double-click insert, a deliberate exception to "a commit never changes the selection" — and the clip cursor stays put, so `⌘/Ctrl+D` right after lays the next copy flush behind them. `⌘/Ctrl+D` (only with notes selected; bare `D` is spare) is this same paste with the selection as the clipboard and the latest selected end as the paste point (`Clip::duplicated_notes`), so the two never disagree on mute, overlaps or the window.

### Clip Edge Drag-Resize

- **Arranger only, press-drag on a clip's left/right edge, decided at press time**: `MouseClicked` hit-tests with `Display::clip_edge_at` *before* the velocity-drag/cursor-placement/marquee logic runs; on a hit, `Display::begin_clip_resize_drag` takes over and the click is *not* forwarded as `MouseClickedTicks` — same "distinct gesture" shape as the velocity drag above. Hover (no button held) is tracked separately in `Display.gesture.clip_resize_hover`, using the same hit-test, and drives the resize cursor icon (`egui::CursorIcon::ResizeHorizontal`, set once per frame in `update()`) — no other visual affordance (a grip glyph adds nothing on top of the cursor change).
- **Only the top `CLIP_HEADER_H`-px header band of the lane is grabbable**: `clip_edge_at` gates the whole hit-test on `y` landing in the lane's top strip (`rendering::clip_resize_band`, the same `CLIP_HEADER_H` the header strip is drawn with — `030-ui-design.md`). A full-lane-height edge hit-test would swallow every press near a shared clip boundary, so a click *between* two touching clips could never place the cursor / start a time selection there. The band clamps to half the lane height on a very short lane so body space always remains below it.
- **Selecting the target clip is the press's job, mirroring a plain click**: for a real clip, `begin_clip_resize_drag` sends a synthesized `MouseClickedTicks` (`SelectTrackAt` + `SetCursorAndSelectClip`) using the clip's own `start_tick` — not the raw press position, which sidesteps the exclusive-end hit-test edge case right at the boundary being grabbed.
- **Drives the "selected clip" region-length mutators by absolute target**: `Display::extend_clip_resize_drag` (driven by `MouseMoved`) snaps the pointer through the same `snap_to_grid` + `cursor_grid_ticks()` path as cursor placement (the zoom-adaptive snap in the Arranger) and sends `InputEvent::ResizeSelectedClipRegionEnd`/`Start { target_tick, drag_id }` on every move, mapped 1:1 to `SequencerCommand::ResizeSelectedClipRegionEnd`/`Start`. Both no-op when no clip is selected. The keyboard equivalent is `[`/`]` (`SetClipEdgeToCursor`, below).
- **Continuous clamp**: the trim calculators (`Sequencer::clip_end_trimmed_to`/`clip_start_trimmed_to`, `region/mod.rs`; they return the new `ClipBounds` and `ResizeClipEdit` applies them) clamp the target continuously to the available space — the edge just stops at the neighbor clip's boundary (`Track::next_clip_start_after`/`prev_clip_end_before`) or the minimum length, rather than refusing to move further. **The minimum is `time::min_clip_length_ticks()` — a beat**, a model invariant, independent of the snap: the snap can be finer (a 16th zoomed in), so a drag snapped on a 16th still stops at one beat of length.
- **Left-edge trim keeps `end_tick` fixed by moving `region().start()` in lockstep with `start_tick`**: shifting a clip's arrangement position without touching its loop-window offset would just move the whole clip, not trim it. Moving both by the same delta keeps the loop's playback phase locked to what's already there. This bounds the drag two ways: the previous clip's end (`Track::prev_clip_end_before`), and however far `region().start()` can retreat toward 0 — past that point the clip has no more original material to reveal, matching how a real DAW refuses to trim past a clip's recorded content. A running-capture commit deliberately leaves material to reveal: the new clip keeps the whole last pass of the take and its region is only the slice from the cursor, so the notes played before the cursor's phase sit before `region().start()` (left edge) and anything past a clamped window sits after `region().end()` (right edge) — see `100-running-capture.md` § *What a new clip retains*. Dragging either way while the transport is running and the playhead is in the gap *before* the clip is safe: `Track::tick` re-seeks a waiting clip whose start moved (`Track::awaiting_start_tick`, `050-undo-redo.md`), so the current wrap stays in phase rather than only the next one.
- **Undoable, one step per drag** (`220-capture-without-pending-view.md`): every move is a `ResizeClipEdit` (`clip_edits/resize_clip.rs`) carrying the drag's id, drawn from `Display.gesture.next_drag_id` at `begin_clip_resize_drag` (the counter the velocity drag uses too). `SequencerEdit::merge` folds consecutive steps with the same id into one: the merged edit keeps the first `before` bounds and takes the latest `after`, so one ⌘Z undoes the whole drag. The result is `EditResult::ClipResized` (`clip_resized_workflow`: `ClipUpdated` plus the lead clip's `EventsUpdated`). No re-seek.
- **Release just drops the drag state**: `MouseReleased` clears `clip_resize_drag`; the merged edit is already on the record.
- **`[`/`]` move a clip edge to the cursor** (`SequencerCommand::SetClipEdgeToCursor(ClipEdge)`, `clip_edges.rs`), undoably, as a keyboard `ResizeClipEdit` that never merges. Any modifier but ⌘/Ctrl, so layouts that type brackets with ⌥ or ⇧ reach them. The two views mean different things, like Ableton's arrangement trim and clip-view start marker:
  - **Arranger: an arrangement trim at the arranger cursor**, exactly the edge drag's clamping (content stays put in time). It acts on the clip under the cursor on the selected track, otherwise the nearest one on the side the edge faces (`Sequencer::arranger_edge_clip_id`: `[` the next clip after the cursor, `]` the last one ending at or before it), so the keys can grow a clip back toward the cursor as well as shrink it. The lead clip then re-syncs to the cursor.
  - **Clip view: a marker on the lead clip at the clip cursor.** `[` moves only the window start to the cursor (`selected_clip_start_marker_at`): the end stays, the clip keeps its arrangement start so the new 1.1.1 lands where the old one was, and the length follows (end − start). The end changes only deliberately, with `]`; the clip stays put in the arrangement and the music under it moves. `]` ends the window at the whole bar count nearest the cursor, at least one bar, floored to the room before the next clip (`selected_clip_end_marker_at`). Both keep a captured clip whole bars (`150-clock-position-sync.md`).
  - **Always at the cursor, playing or not, and never the playhead** (`220`: edits never move the transport). The clip's events don't change, so the current pass plays out as it was and the next loop wrap's seek picks up the new window; an end pulled in behind the playhead leaves it running on past the end until the user restarts.
  - **The project's only clip is exact**: while the project has one clip, `]` on it ends exactly at the cursor in the clip view too; Enter fits the tempo to it (`RetimeClipEdit`), nothing stored in between. With more than one clip, the clip view's `]` rounds up to the bar the cursor is in. While the project has one clip the loop region is that clip, following its edges (`loop_sole_clip_workflow`; the first stopped `/` also turns looping on with `TransportCommand::LoopOver`); with more clips the loop never follows. See `220-capture-without-pending-view.md`. Both `clip_resize_drag` and `clip_resize_hover` are also cleared on `ClipEntered`/`ClipExited`/`ProjectLoaded` (`Display::clear_clip_resize`), alongside `clear_time_selection`/`clear_event_marquee`, so no stale hover/drag survives a view change.

### Clip Band Press & Move Drag

- **Arranger only, press on a clip's header band *off its edges*, decided at press time**: `MouseClicked` hit-tests `Display::clip_band_at` right *after* `clip_edge_at` (and after the `SHIFT+click` arm — a Shift+click on the band extends the marquee to cover the clip instead of pressing it, see "Arranger Time Selection" above) (an edge grab keeps first refusal over the band behind it) and before the velocity-drag/cursor-placement/marquee logic; on a hit, `Display::begin_clip_move_drag` takes over and the click is *not* forwarded as `MouseClickedTicks` — the same "distinct gesture" shape as the edge resize. `clip_band_at` uses the exact `clip_resize_band` gate (`CLIP_HEADER_H`, `030-ui-design.md`) and a half-open `[start_x, end_x)` interior test on that track's shapes; the clip currently being recorded is never grabbable. Hover with no button held is tracked in `Display.gesture.clip_move_hover` and drives `egui::CursorIcon::Grab`; the held press drives `Grabbing` from mouse-down until release (keyed on `clip_move_drag.is_some()`, not on the drag threshold, so a plain click doesn't flash back to the arrow between press and release) — set once per frame in `update()`, after the `ResizeHorizontal` branch so an edge always wins. Consequence: a drag *starting* on a clip's band never starts a marquee — the body below the band, and empty lane, do.
- **The press itself is "select this clip and marquee exactly its span" — unless it lands inside an active marquee, in which case it grabs everything in the marquee (see the next bullet)**: `begin_clip_move_drag` sends `InputEvent::SelectClipSpan { track_idx, clip_id }` → `SequencerCommand::SelectClipSpan` → `select_clip_span_workflow` (`src/core/event_handlers/selection.rs`), which puts the cursor on the clip's `start_tick`, runs `select_track_workflow` + `select_clip_workflow`, then pushes `UiEvent::TimeSelectionSet { start, end, track_span: Some((t, t)) }` — the one-press equivalent of dragging a marquee edge to edge over the clip, so the marquee ops (`⌘/Ctrl+D` Duplicate Clips — the motivating case — `Delete`, `⌘C`/`⌘X`, `M`) get the whole clip with no hand-dragged range. A press that never crosses the drag threshold does exactly this and nothing else. It is a sequencer round-trip rather than a view-local `time_selection` write because the marquee is cursor-anchored: `sync_time_selection_to_cursor` collapses it the frame it sees the cursor or `selected_track_idx` change with no drag anchor set, and this press changes both, asynchronously — a view-set marquee would be wiped a frame later. So the workflow sets cursor + track + clip first and sends the selection *last* on the same FIFO `UiEvent` channel, and the `TimeSelectionSet` handler (`ui_events.rs`) **re-latches** `gesture.last_cursor_tick` / `last_selected_track_idx` to their (already final) values, which makes the whole thing race-free. `TimeSelectionSet` carries `track_span: Option<(usize, usize)>` for this; the `Shift+⌘/Ctrl+D` / Delete-Time-undo senders pass `None` and keep their "preserve the active span" fallback, while plain `⌘/Ctrl+D` passes `Some` like the band press (see above). Behaviour notes: the cursor lands on the clip **start** (a plain lane click puts it at the snapped click tick) — the marquee's anchor edge must sit on the cursor (`nudge_time_selection_edge`), and it is Ableton's "selection start is the insert marker"; `⌘/Ctrl+E` straight after a band press is therefore a no-op (a split exactly on the clip's start boundary is meaningless — the same guard `from_selected_clip` already has), move the cursor first; `Delete` on the resulting marquee removes the clip whole via `DeleteInRangeEdit` (the only way a clip is ever deleted). The in-lane hover cursor line is moved to the clip start immediately at press time (`hover_cursor`) rather than a frame later when the sequencer's cursor move lands.
- **A press inside an active marquee drags everything inside the marquee — tick range × track range — as one block, and keeps the marquee**: decided at press time by the pure `marquee_under_press(time_selection, track_idx, press_tick)` (`gestures.rs`): when `time_selection` is `Some`, has real tick width (`has_tick_range`), its track range covers the pressed clip's track, and the *raw* press tick falls in `[start, end)`, the drag is `ClipMoveKind::Marquee` and **nothing is sent**: no `SelectClipSpan`, no cursor move, the marquee stays exactly as it was (it is the origin tint for the ghost). The dragged block is then the marquee's rect — `ClipMoveDrag.start_tick`/`end_tick`/`track_start`/`track_end` — rather than the clip's bounds on its one track (`ClipMoveKind::Clip`); the pressed clip's id is still kept, in `ClipMoveDrag.pressed_clip_id`, purely for the plain-click case below (whose lane also becomes the lane anchor, `press_track_idx`). A press on a partly-marqueed clip that lands *outside* the marquee's tick range — or on a track outside its track range, or with no marquee at all — is the ordinary press above: the marquee is discarded and replaced by the whole clip's span. This is also the one way to move *part* of a clip: marquee the piece (mouse drag or `⇧←`/`⇧→`), then grab its band inside the marquee — a marquee over one track and part of one clip is just the one-piece block.
- **A plain click (no drag) inside the marquee reselects the clip it landed on, same as a click outside it**: resolved at release, not press, since a `Marquee` press defers "is this a move or a click?" (the bullet above sends nothing at press time, unlike a whole-clip press, which already fires `SelectClipSpan` there — so the marquee case needs its own place to give a plain click its ordinary meaning). `finish_clip_move_drag` checks `!drag.dragging` — exactly "never crossed `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX`", which happens to also mean `!has_moved()` (`dragging` and the ghost's target only ever change together, in `extend_clip_move_drag`/`nudge_clip_move_drag`) — so the move-commit branch and this one are mutually exclusive, never both, never neither for a `Marquee` drag. On a plain click it sends the exact `InputEvent::SelectClipSpan { track_idx: drag.press_track_idx, clip_id }` a press outside the marquee sends immediately: cursor to the clip's start, the clip selected, its span marqueed — collapsing the block selection down to just the one clip clicked, exactly Ableton's behavior for clicking a clip inside an existing selection. A drag that crossed the threshold and returned home (net zero delta) is a completed drag, not a click, and reselects nothing — same as a whole-clip drag returning home.
- **Hold and drag = move; the drag is a purely view-local ghost — nothing in the sequencer changes until release**: `Display.gesture.clip_move_drag: Option<ClipMoveDrag>` (`pointer_state.rs`: the clip, its press-time bounds, the raw `grab_offset_ticks`, the press point, a `dragging` latch, and the live `target_start_tick`/`target_track_idx`) is `Some` only while the button is held, doubling as the drag-active flag. `extend_clip_move_drag` (driven by `MouseMoved`) latches `dragging` once the pointer is `DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX` (3px, shared with the event marquee) off the press point, then recomputes the ghost's absolute target each move — never accumulates — as `snapped_move_start(pointer_tick, grab_offset, grid)` = `snap_to_grid(pointer_tick − grab_offset_ticks, cursor_grid_ticks())` clamped `≥ 0` (same zoom-adaptive arranger snap as cursor placement and the edge resize; keeping the grab offset holds the block under the hand instead of jumping its start to the pointer), and the lane delta as `track_idx_at(y) − press_track_idx` — off the lanes the pointer counts as the press lane (the same wander rule as `extend_time_selection`) — run through `clamped_track_delta` so the *whole* block stays on the lanes (a two-lane block can't be dragged one lane past the bottom). Both kinds move by one `delta_ticks` + `delta_tracks` (`ClipMoveDrag::delta_ticks()`), never per-clip targets. It **sends nothing**. Rendering (`draw_arranger_view`, `030-ui-design.md`): the clip keeps drawing at its real position at full opacity — that is still the data — and a translucent copy (`CLIP_GHOST_ALPHA`) is drawn last, at the target, over whatever it hovers; the marquee from the press stays on the *origin* for the same reason. The ghost (`draw_clip_move_ghost`) is every clip in the block translated by the drag delta — the one clip for `Clip`, every clip on the marqueed lanes overlapping the marquee's ticks for `Marquee` — each painted through `draw_clip_at`'s `window` (its overlap with the block, shifted) so a straddling clip shows just its marqueed piece, with its note thumbnails still lined up to where they sit inside the clip, exactly as the split-out piece will look once dropped. A `Marquee` ghost is additionally framed by `draw_marquee_ghost_frame`: the shifted rect itself, full lane-row height across every lane it spans, as a light `theme::fg_dim()` fill (no outline — it reads as clutter) under the ghost clips — so a selection with gaps, or one reaching past its clips, keeps its shape mid-drag, and with the source marquee still tinted where it is both "from" and "to" read at once (the Logic / Bitwig / Live convention for a dragged selection). No auto-scroll at the viewport edge, no modifier to disable snap, no multi-clip or ⌥-duplicate variant — all could layer on `ClipMoveDrag` later.
- **Release is the single commit point, and it is undoable — unlike the edge resize**: `MouseReleased` → `finish_clip_move_drag`, which sends one event only when the drag latched *and* the ghost sits off the block's own position (`ClipMoveDrag::has_moved` — a non-zero tick or lane delta); a plain press or a drag returned home sends nothing. A `Clip` drag sends `InputEvent::MoveClip { track_idx, clip_id, to_track_idx, to_start_tick }`; a `Marquee` drag sends `InputEvent::MoveRange { rect, delta_ticks, delta_tracks }` — the marquee itself travels in the event because it is view-local state the sequencer has never seen. Release then re-runs the band hover test at the drag's last recorded pointer position (`ClipMoveDrag::last_x`/`last_y` — `MouseReleased` carries no coordinates and the hover was cleared at press), so a plain click goes closed hand → open hand instead of dropping to the arrow until the next `MouseMoved`; after a real move the moved shape only appears under the pointer once `ClipAdded` lands, so there the next `MouseMoved` restores the hand. It carries explicit ids rather than "the selected clip" because the press's selection round-trip is asynchronous. `input_handler.rs` maps both 1:1 (Arranger-gated) to `SequencerCommand::MoveClip` → `MoveClipEdit::new(..)` / `SequencerCommand::MoveRange` → `MoveRangeEdit::new(..)` + `undo_record.edit` (`050-undo-redo.md`). **`MoveClip`**: the clip is lifted off its source track, `DeleteInRangeEdit::from_track_range` carves exactly its destination span out of the destination track (built *after* the lift, so a same-track move over its own old span never carves itself), and the clip is put down there with the same id and an untouched region — a move never trims. Whatever it landed on is trimmed / split / removed Ableton-style, non-destructively like every other carve. **`MoveRange`**: the marquee's tick range is first split out of every clip it overlaps on the marqueed tracks (two track-scoped `SplitClipsEdit`s — at `rect.end`, then `rect.start` — the same non-destructive `⌘E` cut `Delete`/`M` use, so every leftover and piece keeps the full event list and only the region windows differ; a piece keeps its clip's id when it starts at the clip's start, otherwise it is the fresh right-hand product of the split at `rect.start`), then *every* piece is lifted before any carve — so a block shifted by less than its own width never carves its own pieces — and each piece's destination span is carved out of its destination track and the piece dropped there, shifted by the one `(delta_ticks, delta_tracks)`. The resize stays non-undoable because it only touches region length; a move can destroy other clips' spans, so it must be a `SequencerEdit`. Since no data mutates during the drag, each is a single discrete edit — not the per-`MouseMoved` `Edit::merge` pattern the velocity drag needs.
- **After a move (and after its undo) the clip is left exactly as a fresh band press would leave it**: `move_clip_workflow` / `unmove_clip_workflow` (`clip_range_edits.rs`) do `sequencer.reset()`, clear the clip selection, `ClipRemoved` for the old position, the carve buckets (`paste_clips_workflow`'s order forward, `unpaste_clips_workflow`'s on undo), `ClipAdded` for the new one, then re-run `select_clip_span_workflow` on the clip where it now sits — cursor on its start, track + clip selected, span marqueed. Always remove + add even on a same-track move: clip shapes are keyed by `(track_idx, clip_id)` and `update_clip_shape` can't change lanes. The selection is cleared *first* so the reselect is a real state change rather than a no-op on an unchanged id (the lead clip is model-only state — nothing renders it — so this is about `Shift+Tab`/`/` targeting, not pixels). `MoveClipEdit` carries no frozen selection context for the same reason — the clip always survives and is always reselected.
- **After a marquee move the marquee follows the block; after its undo it goes back**: `move_range_workflow` / `unmove_range_workflow` do the same reset + clear, then `ClipRemoved` for every piece at its source (`moved_from` — a no-op for a piece that was split out mid-clip, since the UI never saw that fresh id), the split buckets (`ClipUpdated` for the id-keeping leftovers, `ClipAdded` for the new right-hand ones — forward; `ClipRemoved` for those on undo), the carve buckets, `ClipAdded` for every piece at its destination (forward) / for every re-merged source clip (`restored`, undo — `ClipAdded`'s add-or-replace in `add_clip_shape` covers a leftover shape the UI still has and one that left with its piece alike). Then `select_range_workflow`, the block equivalent of `select_clip_span_workflow`: cursor to `rect.start`, `rect.track_start` selected, the clip under the cursor there selected if it is one of the pieces (forward) / restored originals (undo) — the same cursor-clip rule as `paste_clips_workflow` — and `UiEvent::TimeSelectionSet { .., track_span: Some(..) }` last, with the rect shifted by the delta (`RangeMoved.new_selection`) or the original (`RangeUnmoved.restored_selection`), so the block stays selected where it landed and a second band press inside it moves it on. Sent last for the same re-latch reason as the band press.
- **Esc cancels a live drag; view changes drop it**: the Arranger `Escape` arm calls `clear_clip_move_drag()` beside `clear_time_selection()` — the ghost vanishes, nothing is committed. `clip_move_drag`/`clip_move_hover` are also cleared on `ClipEntered`/`ClipExited`/`ProjectLoaded`, alongside the other `clear_*` drag resets.
- **`⌘/Ctrl+←`/`→` is the keyboard twin of the marquee band drag — horizontal-only, no mouse required — and shares its ghost-preview-then-single-commit shape rather than mutating on every press**: bound in `forward_input_event` beside `⇧←`/`⇧→` (same "the operand is view-local" rationale as `⌘/Ctrl+L`), gated on `modifiers.command && !modifiers.shift && !modifiers.alt` so only the exact chord arms a destructive drag — a stray ⇧ contends with the marquee-*resize* binding, a stray ⌥ with cursor navigation. ⌘/Ctrl rather than ⌥ because the move is the rarer, heavier gesture, while ⌥ is the conventional "coarser cursor step" modifier (word-jump in every text editor) — so ⌥ is reserved for cursor navigation (the `⌥←`/`⌥→` clip-edge jumps). `⇧⌘/Ctrl+←`/`→` is deliberately unbound *and consumed* by its own arm ahead of the `⇧` nudge: it must neither keep nudging as if ⌘ weren't held nor fall through to the handler's plain cursor step. `Display::nudge_clip_move_drag` (`input/gestures.rs`) is the keyboard sibling of `begin_clip_move_drag`/`extend_clip_move_drag`: the first press with a real-tick-width `time_selection` and no drag already in progress arms a `ClipMoveDrag { kind: Marquee, via_keyboard: true, .. }` straight into `dragging: true` (a keypress is already deliberate — unlike a mouse press, there's no "was that just a click?" threshold to cross) with `target_start_tick` one grid step (`cursor_grid_ticks()`) from the marquee's edge; each further press while that same drag is live nudges `target_start_tick` by one more grid step from wherever the ghost *already* sits (`nudged_ghost_start`, clamped `>= 0`) rather than re-deriving from a live pointer tick, since there is no pointer. `delta_tracks` stays `0` for the drag's whole life — no vertical twin, `⌘↑`/`⌘↓` do nothing — **enforced**: while a keyboard-armed drag is live, the first arm of `forward_input_event`'s match consumes every `KeyPressed` except unshifted `←`/`→` (the nudge) and `Escape` (cancel), because ⌘/Ctrl is held for the drag's whole life so every key is a ⌘/Ctrl chord of the gesture, not a separate command — including every real `⌘/Ctrl+X` command (`⌘S`, `⌘Z`, `⌘D`, `⌘L`, …): none of them should fire under a live ghost. It also refuses ⌥-chorded `←`/`→`, which neither the move arm (`!alt`) nor the clip-edge jump arm (`!command`) would claim: reaching the handler's cursor or track keys under a live ghost would move the cursor/`selected_track_idx` and wipe the marquee tint via `sync_time_selection_to_cursor`, while the frozen drag rect still committed on the original tracks — the UI would contradict the move. Ignoring is the standard answer here (a stray key during a modifier-held nudge neither commits nor cancels in any editor; the modifier release is the commit boundary and Esc the cancel), and the keyboard mirror of "the mouse never touches a keyboard-armed drag" below (shifting a selection across tracks is a heavier gesture than "nudge it a little" and stays mouse-only, unlike Ableton, which also has no track-axis arrow-key nudge). This reuses the *exact* ghost + ghost-frame rendering already built for the mouse drag (`draw_clip_move_ghost` keys only on `clip_move_drag.dragging`, blind to `via_keyboard`) — so holding ⌘/Ctrl and tapping `→` repeatedly shows the same translucent preview sliding across whatever it passes over, carving nothing until commit. **Do not commit one `MoveRangeEdit` per keypress**: that carves into whatever the ghost passes over at each intermediate grid step instead of just its final resting position. Photoshop/Illustrator-style modifier-held nudges preview and commit on release, matching this app's own mouse-drag convention.
- **`MoveModifierReleased` is the commit trigger — the keyboard drag's only exit besides Esc**: a new `InputEvent` (`input_event.rs`; named for its role, not the key, so a rebind doesn't ripple), detected in `InputPoller` (`view/input_poller.rs`) the same way `MouseReleased` is — polling `i.modifiers.command` (⌘ on macOS, Ctrl elsewhere — the same key every `⌘/Ctrl+X` binding tests) for a down→up edge each frame, mirroring `left_mouse_down`'s pattern — rather than relying on an `egui::Event`, since egui doesn't surface a modifier's own release as one. `forward_input_event`'s `MoveModifierReleased` arm calls `finish_clip_move_drag()` — the *exact* function `MouseReleased` calls — only when `clip_move_drag.is_some_and(|d| d.via_keyboard)`, so releasing ⌘/Ctrl mid an unrelated *mouse* band drag (a live one always has `via_keyboard: false`) can't stomp on it; it's a no-op the rest of the time, including every other ⌘/Ctrl release in the app (after `⌘S`, `⌘Z`, …). Same guard (`dragging && has_moved()`) as the mouse path, so tapping ⌘/Ctrl with no arrow presses, or presses that net to zero, commits nothing. Esc still cancels a keyboard-armed drag exactly like a mouse one (`clear_clip_move_drag()`, unconditional on view). **The mouse never touches a keyboard-armed drag**: `extend_clip_move_drag` returns early on `via_keyboard`, and the `MouseReleased` arm only calls `finish_clip_move_drag()` when the live drag is *not* `via_keyboard` — the mirror of the `MoveModifierReleased` guard. Without the first, a nudge's ghost (armed straight into `dragging`, so past the threshold check) would be yanked to the pointer by the first stray mouse motion while ⌘/Ctrl is held; without the second, a click during a nudge would commit it on release. Standard practice (Photoshop/Illustrator nudges, DAW keyboard moves): the pointer only drives a drag the *button* started, and a keyboard move is owned by the keyboard until its own commit/cancel. The pointer icon follows the same rule: the `Grabbing` closed hand in `update()` and the hover no-op in `update_clip_move_hover` are both keyed on a live drag that is *not* `via_keyboard`, so during a nudge the pointer stays an arrow and still shows the open hand over a band, exactly as if no drag were in progress. If a fresh mouse gesture is started while a keyboard nudge's ghost is still showing (⌘/Ctrl held, no arrow press since), `begin_clip_move_drag` simply overwrites `clip_move_drag` — the pending nudge is silently discarded uncommitted, not merged or force-committed first; a narrow, physically awkward edge case (holding ⌘/Ctrl while separately mouse-pressing) judged not worth the extra state to handle specially, since nothing has actually mutated yet at that point either way. `KeyModifiers::alt` exists for this; the plugin-editor key guard has no alt key of its own and always reports `false`.
- **The `MoveModifierReleased` arm also calls `ctx.request_repaint()` — load-bearing, not decorative**: sending `InputEvent::MoveRange` only *starts* the commit; the sequencer thread applies it and mails back the `ClipRemoved`/`ClipAdded`/`TimeSelectionSet` `UiEvent`s that actually move the drawn shapes and the marquee, and `Display` only drains those on its *next* frame. The mouse-driven commit has the identical round trip and the identical one-frame lag — "after a real move the moved shape only appears under the pointer once `ClipAdded` lands, so there the next `MouseMoved` restores the hand" (above) — but it's invisible there because releasing a mouse button after a drag is essentially always followed by more pointer motion, which forces the next frame anyway. Releasing ⌘/Ctrl has no such guaranteed follow-up: with the pointer stationary and no other input pending, egui's reactive loop would otherwise sit idle for however long — the ghost gone, the content still drawn at its pre-move position — reading as "the move was cancelled," until some unrelated later input (a click, an arrow press, moving the mouse) happened to wake a frame and reveal it had actually landed all along. `forward_input_event` takes a `ctx: &egui::Context` parameter for this (threaded from `handle_input_events`, its one caller) — the same `EventHandlers::request_repaint()` idiom used for every other background-round-trip wake in this codebase, just called directly since this runs on the UI thread inside a live frame already, with no need for the cross-thread `repaint_ctx` indirection that idiom exists for.

### Track Header Volume / Pan Drag

- **Arranger only, press-drag on an instrument track's header bar, decided at press time**: `MouseClicked` hit-tests with `Display::track_mix_bar_at` **before** `clip_edge_at` / velocity / cursor logic — most specific first — and on a hit the click is *not* forwarded as `MouseClickedTicks`. The press deliberately does **not** change the selected track or move the cursor: touching a fader is not a navigation gesture. Bars only exist for tracks where `Display::track_has_instrument` is true (a CLAP plugin editor is loaded); off macOS there are none.
- **⌘/Ctrl+click on a bar resets it** to neutral (`0.0` dB / centre) and starts no drag — resolved inline in `MouseClicked` from the `modifiers` it already carries. No conflict with the ⌘+drag velocity gesture, which is clip-view only.
- **View-local drag state**: `Display.gesture.track_mix_drag: Option<TrackMixDrag>` (`Some` only while the primary button is held — doubles as the drag-active flag, like every other drag here) carrying `track_idx`, `param: TrackMixParam` (`Volume`/`Pan`), the press `anchor_y`, the parameter's `anchor_value` at press time, and `last_sent`. `Display.gesture.track_mix_hover: Option<(usize, TrackMixParam)>` is the hover-only counterpart, driving `egui::CursorIcon::ResizeVertical` (set once per frame in `update()`, beside the `ResizeHorizontal` branch).
- **`extend_track_mix_drag(y, shift)` recomputes an absolute value from the anchor** — never accumulates deltas (the drift rule every extender follows) — quantises it (0.1 dB / 1 pan unit) and sends `InputEvent::SetTrackVolume`/`SetTrackPan` only when it changed since `last_sent`. `TRACK_MIX_DRAG_PX_FULL_TRAVEL` (240px) spans the whole travel; **holding Shift divides the rate by 5** for fine adjustment (this is why `MouseMoved` carries `modifiers`). Not gated on `is_mouse_inside_clip_content` — wandering off the bar mustn't cancel a live drag.
- **`input_handler.rs` maps the two `InputEvent`s 1:1** onto `SequencerCommand::SetTrackVolume`/`SetTrackPan`, Arranger-gated. The handler applies them as **direct mutations of `SharedAtomics.track_mix`** — no `undo_record.edit`, no `EditResult` — exactly like `SetTrackOutput` and matching the region-length precedent in `050-undo-redo.md`.
- **Release just drops the drag state**: `MouseReleased` clears `track_mix_drag`; the value stays. `track_mix_drag`/`track_mix_hover` are also cleared on `ClipEntered`/`ClipExited`/`ProjectLoaded` (`Display::clear_track_mix_drag`), alongside the other `clear_*` drag resets.

### Track Header Mute / Solo Buttons

- **Arranger only, a plain click on the S (solo) or M (mute) button in a track header**: `MouseClicked` hit-tests `Display::track_button_at` **first of all** — ahead of `track_mix_bar_at` and `clip_edge_at`, most specific first — and on a hit sends `InputEvent::ToggleTrackSolo`/`ToggleTrackMute { track_idx }` and returns without forwarding `MouseClickedTicks`. Like the fader press, this deliberately does **not** select the track or move the cursor.
- **Shown on every track, unlike the volume/pan bars**: mute/solo gate note emission in `Sequencer::tick`, which covers external MIDI-out tracks too, so `track_button_at` loops every track with no `track_has_instrument` filter. The button row is still laid out by the shared `track_header_rects` geometry, so it lands at the same y in every lane whether or not that track shows bars below it.
- **View-local hover state only**: `Display.gesture.track_button_hover: Option<(usize, TrackButton)>` (`TrackButton` = `Solo`/`Mute`/`Output`, the last being the output chip below) drives `egui::CursorIcon::PointingHand` (set once per frame in `update()`, beside the `ResizeVertical` branch) and is cleared in `Display::clear_track_mix_drag` alongside the mix-bar hover/drag. There is no click-drag — a click is instantaneous — so there is no `TrackButtonDrag`.
- **`input_handler.rs` maps the two `InputEvent`s 1:1** onto `SequencerCommand::ToggleTrackMute`/`ToggleTrackSolo`, Arranger-gated. The handler flips the flag in `SharedAtomics.track_mix` (`mute`/`solo`, `[AtomicBool; MAX_TRACKS]`) as a **direct mutation** — no `undo_record.edit`, no `EditResult` — exactly like the volume/pan drag and `ToggleMetronomeMute`. Audibility is the pure `mix::track_is_audible(muted, soloed, any_solo)` (`core::audio::mix`) — mute wins over solo on the same track — derived at the point of use, never stored.
- **Silencing is immediate and total, both routes**: on a change that silences a track mid-playback the sequencer calls `release_track_notes`, which queues that track's currently-sounding clip note-offs (the `Track::pending_note_offs` path clip-mute uses) so nothing hangs — notes held on the physical keyboard are untouched. For hosted-instrument tracks that only stops *new* sound; the **reverb / delay tail** is cut by `InstrumentMixer::target_gains` forcing that track's output gain to `0.0` while it is inaudible (ramped out over one buffer, click-free). External MIDI-out tracks get only the note-offs — their tail lives in outboard gear the app can't reach.

### Track Header Output Chip & Menu

Where a track's output goes — MIDI out on a channel, or a hosted plugin — is set from the track header (replaced the F1 `Track` modal, 2026-10-04). Putting a plugin *on* a track is the browser panel's (§ Views); everything else is here. Mouse-only, by decision: a keyboard chord (say ⌘⌫, remove the instrument) only if it's missed.

- **The chip** sits on the S/M row, right of M (`TrackHeaderRects::output`, `030` § Arranger Track Header), on every track and every platform: `Ch 3` on a MIDI track, the plugin's name on a plugin track — in italics when the plugin isn't loaded (not installed, or the project opened off macOS). A press on it opens that track's menu (`TrackButton::Output` → `Display::open_output_menu`); like S/M it neither selects the track nor moves the cursor.
- **The view mirrors the track list**: `Display.tracks: Vec<TrackLane>`, one per track, each `{ slot, route, color, name }` — its engine slot (`Track::slot`, where its mix values and plugin live: the header reads its faders and the plugin host its editor by slot), `TrackRoute` (`MidiOut { channel }` / `Instrument { name }` — `TrackOutput` without the plugin reference and its state) and its colour slot (`Track::color_slot`, what `Display::track_color` draws it in). Replaced wholesale by `UiEvent::TracksChanged { tracks, shift }`, which `EventHandlers::emit_tracks` (`event_handlers/tracks.rs`) sends after every `SetTrackOutput`, every track add / remove, every rename (and its undo) and on project load / new-project. Its length is the view's track count (`Display::track_count()`), which the arranger's lanes, hit-tests and marquee clamps read. Each lane also carries the track's `name` (`Track::name`, `None` while unnamed), which the header's name row draws (§ Track Rename). Before the first one it holds the fresh-sequencer defaults (`TrackLane::defaults`, `DEFAULT_TRACK_COUNT` tracks, track *n* in slot *n* on channel *n*). `shift` (see § Tracks and Regions) is `None` except after an add / remove.
- **The menu** (`view/display/output_menu.rs`, pure `output_menu_layout` + `OutputMenuLayout::hit_at`, unit-tested; drawn by `rendering/output_menu.rs` over everything but the modals): opens under the chip (above it near the window's bottom edge). A plugin track gets two rows first — the plugin's name with its format (`VST3`/`CLAP`, or `not loaded`) and **Remove <plugin>** — then, on every track, a 4×4 **MIDI channel** grid, the current channel lit. **Remove** routes the track to MIDI out on its own channel (track *n* → channel *n*; a plugin track keeps no channel to return to); a channel cell routes it to that channel, removing the plugin if there is one. Either way `Display::route_track_to_midi` tears the plugin down (`remove_track_instrument`, macOS) and sends `InputEvent::SetTrackOutput { track, output: MidiOut { channel } }` → `SequencerCommand::SetTrackOutput`; removing a plugin is **not undoable** (like replacing one from the browser) and its preset state goes — the footer's passing message says "Removed X from track N, MIDI channel C". Picking the channel the track already has does nothing. Last, under a divider, on every track: **Rename** — opens the rename field on the menu's track (§ Track Rename), as ⌘R does on the selected one — then **Delete track** — `InputEvent::RemoveTrack { track_idx: Some(track) }`, the same undoable `RemoveTrackEdit` as `Delete` in track header focus but for the menu's track. **Delete track** is not there at all on the last track, which can't be deleted (`output_menu_layout`'s `deletable`).
- **Closing**: open-state is `Display.gesture.output_menu: Option<usize>` (the track). While it is open, `handle_output_menu_input_event` gets first look at every event in `handle_input_events`, ahead of every other keymap. It takes every press: an item acts and closes the menu, the menu's title/label/padding do nothing, a press anywhere else closes it and goes no further (the click-away convention — the press doesn't also act on what's under it). Any key closes it; ESC does nothing else, every other key then acts as usual. The pointing hand over an item is derived each frame from the pointer and the layout (`is_pointer_on_output_menu_item`), not stored.

### Track Rename

A track's name (`Track::name: Option<String>`, persisted — `060`) shows on the top row of its header (`030` § Arranger Track Header); unnamed, the row shows the track's number, its position, so it follows a remove above it. Renaming is an inline text field over that row (`view/display/track_rename.rs`), view-local until it commits.

- **In**: `⌘/Ctrl+R` in the arranger (the selected track — bound in `Display`, ahead of bare `R`), a double-click on a header's name row (`Display::track_name_at`; its first press already selected the track and focused the header column, § Views), or the output menu's **Rename**. The field (`Display.track_rename: Option<TrackRename>`) holds the track by position, the text, and who had the keyboard before (`return_focus`), handed back when it closes.
- **The field** is an egui `TextEdit` (caret, selection, IME, clipboard — the app's only egui widget besides Save As's name field, `060`, and the header's BPM field, `030`), shown by `show_track_rename` in the canvas `ui` over `Display::track_name_rect` — the same rect the name is drawn in, so the text doesn't jump (`TRACK_NAME_FONT_SIZE`). Its first frame takes the egui focus and selects the whole text: the track's name, or empty with its number as the hint while unnamed. At most `TRACK_NAME_MAX_CHARS` (40).
- **Keys**: while it is open, `handle_track_rename_input_event` gets first look at every event, ahead of the output menu and every keymap. It takes **every** key — nothing falls through (Delete doesn't remove the track, Space doesn't play, `R` doesn't record) — and the clipboard intents; egui's field reads the same frame's typing on its own. `Enter` commits, `Esc` cancels (the pure `field_event` — keys, clipboard intents and presses on or off the field — unit-tested, shared with the header's BPM field, `030` § Header & Footer).
- **Out**: `Enter` or a press anywhere off the field commits — the press then acts as usual (unlike the output menu's click-away); a press on the field is the field's. `Esc` cancels. A track add / remove (`follow_track_shift`) cancels it, and so — checked once a frame before input, `drop_stale_track_rename` — does its row leaving the screen (the track gone, the arranger hidden) or the arranger losing the keyboard; then the header focus it hands back drops too, as the header focus always does. One rule decides where the field is and whether it is valid (`rename_field_rect`), shared by opening, drawing, the click test and that check, so the field never holds the keyboard while it can't be seen.
- **Commit**: the text goes through `track_name_from_input` (`models/track.rs`, unit-tested: trimmed, cut to the limit, blank → `None`, the number again); then `InputEvent::RenameTrack { track_idx, name }` → `SequencerCommand::RenameTrack` → one undoable `RenameTrackEdit` (`050`) — none for an unchanged name, which the edit alone decides (`RenameTrackEdit::new`); its `EditResult::TrackRenamed` re-sends the mirror (`TracksChanged`, no shift).
